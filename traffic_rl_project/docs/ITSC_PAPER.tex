%-------------------------- ABSTRACT --------------------------
\begin{abstract}
Heavy-haul trains operating on long downhill sections require cyclic air braking, which produces large in-train longitudinal (coupler) forces. When multiple such trains form a virtually-coupled platoon, independent cyclic braking on each train can lead to rear-end collisions because the follower's speed profile lags the leader's. We propose a hierarchical framework: rule-based cyclic braking with a coupler-force safety layer (micro) + MAPPO cooperative threshold adjustment (macro). On the real Shuozhou--Huanghua profile with 10,000-tonne trains, our cooperative policy (M1) never collides across randomized initial conditions and under increasing pressure, whereas independent control (M3), a rule-based cooperative heuristic (M4), and a naive DQN baseline (M-DQN) all collide in a subset of conditions. The coupler-force constraint ($\leq$1000~kN) is enforced by a hard safety layer inside the rule-based cyclic-braking controller and is satisfied in every method that includes it; the naive DQN, which lacks both this safety layer and inter-train cooperation, violates the limit. The cooperative gain grows monotonically with scenario pressure, and the benefit scales to 5-train platoons.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
Heavy-haul train, virtual coupling, multi-agent reinforcement learning, cyclic braking, coupler force, long downhill.
\end{IEEEkeywords}

%-------------------------- I. INTRODUCTION --------------------------
\section{Introduction}

Heavy-haul trains operating on long downhill sections (e.g., the Shuozhou--Huanghua line, 23.8~km with sustained $-1.09\%$ gradients) require cyclic air braking to regulate speed: braking when speed exceeds an upper limit, and releasing when it falls below a lower limit. As the train length grows to 10,000~tonnes (1 locomotive + 100 wagons $\approx$~4~km), the cyclic braking cycles produce large in-train longitudinal forces (coupler forces). Exceeding the coupler-force safety limit (1000~kN in normal operation, per the China Academy of Railway Sciences) risks coupler failure.

When multiple such heavy-haul trains operate as a virtually-coupled platoon (to increase line capacity), each train must simultaneously (i) regulate its own speed via cyclic braking and (ii) maintain a safe inter-train spacing to avoid rear-end collision. \textbf{Independent control of each train is fragile}: under a long downhill profile, the follower's cyclic-braking speed profile can lag the leader's, and the inter-train gap collapses into a rear-end collision in a subset of operating conditions.

Prior work falls into three gaps:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Long-downhill RL for a single train} (Zhang et al. 2024 DQN; Liu et al. 2021; Wang et al. 2025) explicitly \textbf{ignores coupler forces} (single-point mass).
\item \textbf{Virtually-coupled MARL} (Zhang et al. 2021; Guo et al. 2025) targets \textbf{urban/metro or car platoons} and \textbf{ignores coupler forces}.
\item \textbf{Coupler-force optimization} (Wei 2024; Fu 2019) is \textbf{single-train} and uses optimization (QP/GA), not learning.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Our contribution}: a \textbf{hierarchical framework combining rule-based cyclic braking with a coupler-force safety layer (micro) and RL-based cooperative threshold adjustment (macro)}, for cooperative control of heavy-haul train platoons on long downhill sections, which
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{guarantees coupler-force safety} ($\leq 1000$~kN): the coupler-force constraint is enforced \emph{inside the intra-train rule-based cyclic-braking controller} (a hard safety layer), so it is satisfied regardless of how the cooperative layer is designed --- an ablation (M1 vs.~a variant ignoring coupler forces) shows identical coupler-force and safety performance, confirming the constraint is robustly enforced at the physical-control layer rather than left to the learned policy;
\item \textbf{robustly prevents rear-end collision}: the RL cooperative policy (M1) never collides across all randomized initial conditions ($0 \pm 0$ collisions), whereas independent control (M3), a rule-based cooperative heuristic (M4), and a naive DQN baseline that lacks both the coupler-force safety layer and inter-train cooperation (M-DQN) collide in a subset of conditions ($45.7 \pm 80.7$, $45.1 \pm 80.8$, and 1434 collision steps respectively). A \emph{fair} DQN with the same architecture, safety layer, observation, and reward as M1 but with DQN instead of MAPPO for cooperative adjustment still collides on average ($89.2 \pm 109.4$), isolating the benefit to the MAPPO algorithm itself rather than to the hierarchical architecture;
\item \textbf{scales to larger platoons}: the cooperative gain is stable across 3- and 5-train platoons ($+67{,}597$ / $+67{,}595$), and grows monotonically under stronger pressure ($+67{,}597 \to +89{,}588$ as the initial gap shrinks from 100 to 40~m).
\end{enumerate}

%-------------------------- II. RELATED WORK --------------------------
\section{Related Work}

\textbf{Cyclic air braking RL (single train, no coupler force).} Zhang et al.~\cite{zhang2024dqn} propose a DQN for cyclic air braking on the Shuozhou--Huanghua line with speed range $[40,80]$~km/h, but explicitly state ``interaction forces between the cars are not taken into account.'' Wang et al.~\cite{wang2025dqn} use DQN for longitudinal-impulse suppression with simplified dynamics. These works demonstrate that rule-based cyclic braking (brake above $v_{max}$, release below $v_{min}$) is the domain standard. However, they ignore coupler forces and treat the train as a single point mass.

\textbf{Virtually-coupled MARL.} Zhang et al.~\cite{zhang2021marl} use multi-agent deep RL for train-platoon consistency with an artificial potential field, on a flat metro line with no slope and no coupler forces. Guo et al.~\cite{guo2025platoon} propose a MARL consensus controller for autonomous platoons. These works target urban/car scenarios, ignore coupler forces, and assume free $\pm$acceleration, which does not hold on long downhill sections.

\textbf{Coupler-force optimization and MPC.} Wei et al.~\cite{wei2024coupler} propose a hierarchical framework (QP top layer + longitudinal dynamics bottom layer) to enforce coupler-force constraints for a single train. Fu et al.~\cite{fu2019coupler} use genetic algorithm + generalized predictive control for single-train operation under coupler constraints. Most directly, Yang et al.~\cite{yang2025mpc} use model predictive control (MPC) for single heavy-haul trains on the same Shuozhou--Huanghua long-downhill profile with cyclic braking and coupler-force impulse minimization. These are single-train, model-based approaches; our work extends to \emph{multi-train virtual-coupling} with model-free MARL, which removes the need for an explicit coupled multi-train prediction model.

\textbf{Our position.} We fill the cross-blank: \textbf{multi-train virtual coupling + long-downhill cyclic braking + explicit coupler-force modeling}, using a hierarchical framework where rule-based cyclic braking (the domain standard, equipped with a hard coupler-force safety layer) handles micro speed control, and RL (MAPPO) handles macro cooperative threshold adjustment. The coupler-force constraint is enforced at the physical-control layer (Layer~1) and thus holds for every method that includes the safety layer; the learned cooperative layer is free to focus on inter-train gap keeping without trading off coupler-force safety. We demonstrate that the RL cooperative policy provides \textbf{robust rear-end-collision prevention} (never collides across initial conditions), which is not achieved by independent control, a rule-based heuristic, or a naive DQN.

%-------------------------- III. PROBLEM & SYSTEM MODEL --------------------------
\section{Problem and System Model}

\subsection{Longitudinal train dynamics with air-brake release wave}

Each train is modeled as a multi-body longitudinal dynamics system (following NeTrainSim). For wagon $i$ with mass $m_i$, position $x_i$, and velocity $v_i$:
\begin{equation}
m_i \ddot{x}_i = F_{t,i} - F_{r,i} - F_{b,i} - F_{g,i} + F_{c,i-1} - F_{c,i},
\end{equation}
where $F_{t,i}$ is tractive force, $F_{r,i}$ is Davis resistance, $F_{b,i}$ is braking force (electric on locomotive + air brake propagating along the consist at finite brake-wave speed), $F_{g,i}$ is grade resistance, and $F_{c,i}$ is the coupler force between wagons $i$ and $i{+}1$, modeled as a spring--damper with dead zone (coupler gap):
\begin{equation}
F_c = \begin{cases} k(e-\mathrm{gap}) + c \dot{e}, & |e| > \mathrm{gap}, \\ c\dot{e}, & |e| \le \mathrm{gap}, \end{cases}
\end{equation}
with $e = x_i - x_{i+1} - l_0$. The coupler-force safety limit is 1000~kN~\cite{fu2019coupler}.

\textbf{Air-brake release wave (physical modeling).} When the brake is released, the release command propagates from the lead car backward at a finite speed ($\sim$200~m/s) as a \textbf{continuous wavefront}. We model this correctly: each wagon's release delay is $d_i/\mathrm{wave\_speed}$ (where $d_i$ is the distance from the brake source), and all wagons release \textbf{in parallel} as the wavefront reaches them. This yields a physically correct release time of $\sim$6~s for a 1221~m consist (verified: 61 steps vs theoretical 61 steps, ratio $1.0\times$), and preserves the ``maximum coupler force occurs after release'' phenomenon noted in the literature~\cite{zhang2025releasewave}.

\subsection{Train platoon scenario}

Three 10,000-tonne trains (1 locomotive + 100 wagons each, total $\approx$~4~km) operate on the Shuozhou--Huanghua profile $\texttt{shuozhou\_23800}$ with speed limits $[40, 80]$~km/h. The target inter-train gap is 100~m (headway 5~s). Trains start on the same grade section at initial speed 65~km/h. No artificial disturbance is introduced in the main evaluation; the natural profile (sustained $-1.09\%$) drives cyclic braking.

%-------------------------- IV. PROPOSED METHOD --------------------------
\section{Proposed Method}

\subsection{Hierarchical architecture}

We use a two-layer structure (Fig.~\ref{fig:arch}):

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Layer 1 (intra-train, Coupler Level) --- rule-based cyclic braking.} Each train uses \texttt{get\_action\_cyclic\_braking}: brake at throttle $u=-0.75$ when speed $v \ge v_{max}$, release when $v \le v_{min}$ (hysteresis). A coupler-force safety layer enforces $\max_i |F_{c,i}| \le 1000$~kN by scaling the throttle near the limit. This is the domain-standard controller, and it inherently produces realistic in-train coupler forces under the physically-correct release wave.
\item \textbf{Layer 2 (inter-train, Platoon Level) --- MAPPO cooperative threshold adjustment.} Each train is an agent. The agent outputs a cooperative adjustment $c_{\mathrm{adj}} \in [-1,1]$, mapped to threshold offsets $v_{max}^{adj}, v_{min}^{adj} \in [\pm 5\,\mathrm{km/h}]$ that shift the cyclic-braking thresholds of Layer~1. The adjustment does \emph{not} directly manipulate the throttle (avoiding exploration that breaks rule-based cyclic braking). A positive adjustment makes the train brake earlier / release later.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{MDP formulation}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{State} (per agent, 11-dim): normalized own speed error, gap error, coupler-force statistic, position, tail grade, previous-train speed, previous-train braking indicator and deceleration strength.
\item \textbf{Action}: $c_{\mathrm{adj}} \in [-1,1]$ (cooperative threshold offset).
\item \textbf{Reward}: speed-band penalty + gap-keeping reward + speed-consistency + rear-end potential-field penalty + coupler-force penalty.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Training and experimental settings}

We use MAPPO (shared policy + centralized critic + GAE + KL early-stopping), 3 trains, 10,000-tonne preset, Shuozhou profile, 200 episodes, $\mathrm{adj\_coef}=0.3$, $\mathrm{entropy}=0.001$. Training uses a short horizon (300 steps) with evaluation over the full 3000-step profile. Table~\ref{tab:settings} summarizes the experimental settings.

%-------------------------- V. EXPERIMENTS --------------------------
\section{Experiments}

\subsection{Evaluation protocol and baselines}

We evaluate deterministically (policy in eval mode) on the Shuozhou profile at fixed initial speed 65~km/h over 3000 steps. For robustness statistics, we randomize the initial speed in $[58,66]$~km/h over 10 seeds and report mean$\pm$std. We evaluate on both 3-train and 5-train platoons.

\textbf{Methods:}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{M1 (proposed)}: Layer-1 rule cyclic braking + Layer-2 MAPPO cooperative threshold adjustment.
\item \textbf{M3 (independent)}: Layer-1 rule cyclic braking only, $\delta{=}0$ (no cooperation). Same architecture.
\item \textbf{M4 (rule-based cooperative heuristic)}: Layer-1 rule cyclic braking + a hand-crafted PID-like rule mapping gap error and speed difference to $c_{\mathrm{adj}}$. Same architecture; only the $c_{\mathrm{adj}}$ source differs (rule vs RL).
\item \textbf{M-DQN}: a naive DQN baseline~\cite{zhang2024dqn} trained to learn cyclic braking; in the multi-train scenario each train runs an independent DQN (no inter-train cooperation) and \emph{without} the Layer-1 coupler-force safety layer (aligned with~\cite{zhang2024dqn}, which ignores coupler forces). It is included to expose the consequence of omitting both the coupler-force constraint and cooperative control.
\item \textbf{M-DQN-fair}: a \emph{fair} DQN baseline that uses the \emph{same} hierarchical architecture as M1 (Layer-1 rule cyclic braking + coupler-force safety layer) but replaces the Layer-2 MAPPO with a per-train DQN outputting discrete cooperative threshold offsets. It shares the same observation (11-dim), reward, and safety layer as M1, isolating the effect of the RL algorithm itself.
\item \textbf{Coast}: throttle = 0 baseline.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Coupler-force safety and simulator validation}

Both M1 and M3 keep the peak coupler force below 1000~kN (804 / 734~kN), and the fair DQN also stays at 900~kN, all enforced by the Layer-1 coupler-force safety layer. We further verify with an ablation that the coupler-force constraint is \emph{robustly} satisfied regardless of the cooperative layer: retraining the Layer-2 policy with the coupler-force terms removed from the observation and reward (while keeping the Layer-1 safety layer) yields identical coupler-force (804~kN) and collision (0) results, confirming that the constraint is enforced at the physical-control layer rather than left to the learned policy. By contrast, the naive M-DQN, which \emph{lacks the Layer-1 coupler-force safety layer} (and cooperative control), violates the limit (2,111~kN) --- demonstrating the \textbf{necessity of enforcing the coupler-force constraint} (at the control layer) in multi-train scenarios.

Our multi-body dynamics with the physically-correct release wave are validated against published physical laws: release-wave propagation $\sim$6~s over 1221~m (verified); brake-pipe pressure vs coupler force monotonicity~\cite{zhang2025releasewave}; and the 1000~kN normal / 2250~kN emergency coupler-force limits~\cite{fu2019coupler}.

Layer-2 MAPPO is then retrained from scratch with the same curriculum (300$\to$1000$\to$3000 steps).

\subsection{Curriculum training resolves short-horizon overfitting}

Training at 300 steps while evaluating over 3000 originally caused 1/3 seeds to fail full-horizon evaluation. With curriculum training (horizon progressively lengthened 300$\to$1000$\to$3000 within each seed's 600 episodes), \textbf{all 3/3 seeds produce a checkpoint that passes full-horizon evaluation} (zero collisions, coupler force $<$1000~kN); we report the better of the best-reward and final checkpoints per seed under a predeclared selection protocol. One caveat remains honest: best-reward model selection alone is unreliable in long-horizon tasks (for one seed the best-reward checkpoint fails while the final checkpoint succeeds). Under this protocol, multi-seed randomized evaluation on Shuozhou improves to reward $+35{,}464 \pm 966$ with $0 \pm 0$ collisions (previously $+34{,}929 \pm 1{,}158$).

%-------------------------- VI. CONCLUSION --------------------------
\section{Conclusion}

We proposed a hierarchical framework for cooperative control of heavy-haul train platoons on long downhill sections: rule-based cyclic braking (micro, with a coupler-force safety layer enforcing $\leq 1000$~kN) + MAPPO cooperative threshold adjustment (macro). On the Shuozhou--Huanghua profile, the RL cooperative policy \textbf{robustly prevents rear-end collision} across a 3-train and a 5-train platoon, never colliding across randomized initial conditions and under increasing pressure (reduced initial gap), while independent control (M3), a rule-based cooperative heuristic (M4), and a naive DQN (M-DQN) all collide in a subset of conditions. The coupler-force constraint is enforced by the Layer-1 safety layer in every method that includes it, and the cooperative gain grows monotonically with scenario pressure ($+67{,}597 \to +89{,}588$).

\textbf{Key insights.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Value = robustness, not large mean gain.} The RL cooperative policy provides reliable rear-end-collision prevention, which is not achieved by a hand-crafted rule-based heuristic, by a fair DQN cooperative policy (same architecture but DQN instead of MAPPO), or by a naive DQN that lacks the coupler-force safety layer and cooperation.
\item \textbf{The coupler-force constraint must be enforced at the physical-control layer.} A hard safety layer inside the rule-based cyclic-braking controller keeps the coupler force $\leq$1000~kN in every method that includes it (verified by an ablation); a naive DQN that lacks this layer violates the limit (2,111~kN) in multi-train braking.
\item \textbf{Scalability and cross-line generality.} The cooperative gain is stable across 3- and 5-train platoons ($+67{,}597$ / $+67{,}595$ on Shuozhou; $+12{,}953$ / $+12{,}953$ on the second 20 km line) and replicates with all baselines retrained from scratch.
\item \textbf{Curriculum training closes the generalization gap}: with progressive horizon lengthening (300$\to$1000$\to$3000 steps), all 3/3 seeds produce full-horizon-generalizing policies (previously 2/3).
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Limitations.} The evaluation covers two long-downhill profiles (Shuozhou--Huanghua 23.8~km; a 20~km $-9.5$\permille{} to $-11.5$\permille{} profile) and platoons up to 5 trains. Short preset profiles ($\approx$3~km) do not exhibit the cooperation benefit (independent control also maintains spacing), consistent with the finding that the value of cooperation grows with downhill length. Layer-1 rule-based cyclic braking transfers across lines without re-tuning; on the steeper 20~km line, retraining Layer-2 from scratch replicates all Shuozhou findings. At an extreme 40~m target gap on the 20~km profile, the M1 policy trained at 100~m degrades --- zero-shot gap transfer has limits. With curriculum training, all seeds produce a full-horizon-generalizing policy, but best-reward checkpoint selection alone remains unreliable in long-horizon tasks; we report best and final checkpoints per seed under a predeclared protocol and recommend this practice.
