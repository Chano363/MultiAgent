%-------------------------- ABSTRACT --------------------------
\begin{abstract}
Heavy-haul trains on long downhill sections (Shuozhou--Huanghua, 23.8~km,
$-10.9$\permille) rely on minute-scale cyclic air braking; when several trains
form a virtually-coupled platoon, each must follow the cyclic-braking rhythm
while keeping a safe inter-train gap and coupler force within limits. Model
predictive control (MPC) is the industrial incumbent for this regime, but we
argue it carries four \emph{structural} limitations here: fragile
finite-horizon/terminal feasibility, inability to reuse driving experience,
dependence on an extremely precise multi-body model, and weak generalization
across trains and lines. We demonstrate empirically that a \emph{fair}
multi-train MPC with the full coupler and gap model ``sees'' only a $\approx
3.2$~s rolling window and cannot anticipate the minute-level brake--release
rhythm: under a randomized-initialization protocol it stalls below the release
limit for 71.6\% of the run, exceeds the coupler limit (1{,}178--1{,}431~kN),
and collapses into rear-end collision (330 collision steps, reward
$-258{,}831{\pm}116{,}023$). A model-free hierarchical multi-agent
reinforcement learning policy (M-MARL, rule-based cyclic braking with a hard
coupler/ATP safety layer as the inner control layer + MAPPO cooperative
thresholds as the outer layer) holds spacing and coupler force and never
collides (reward $+36{,}004$, $0$ collisions), and generalizes across
randomized initial conditions, 5-train platoons, and a second $20$~km profile
($+36{,}120$, $0$ collisions). We map each MPC limitation to a concrete MARL
advantage and a controlled head-to-head result.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
Heavy-haul train, virtual coupling, model predictive control, multi-agent

%-------------------------- I. INTRODUCTION --------------------------
\section{Introduction}

Heavy-haul trains operating on long downhill sections (e.g., the
Shuozhou--Huanghua line, $23.8$ km with sustained $-10.9$\permille gradients)
must regulate speed through \emph{cyclic air braking}: braking whenever speed
exceeds an upper limit and releasing when it falls below a lower limit. The
minute-scale ``brake--release'' cycles, combined with multi-body coupler
dynamics, produce large in-train longitudinal (coupler) forces that must stay
below the safety limit (1000~kN normal / 2250~kN emergency). When several such
trains operate as a virtually-coupled platoon to raise line capacity, each unit
must additionally keep a safe inter-train spacing while following the
cyclic-braking rhythm. This couples \emph{long-horizon, nonlinear, coupled,
switched} dynamics (air-brake wave propagation, brake--release hysteresis,
varying consist and grade) with a \emph{safety-critical} spacing constraint.

MPC is the incumbent, industrially applied method for train operation and,
more recently, virtual coupling: Yang et al.\ (ITSC 2025) use single-train MPC
on this same Shuozhou profile to minimize coupler impulse; Wang et al.\ (2026)
apply Kalman-filtered MPC to cooperative heavy-haul virtual coupling
(inter-train gap error $\leq 20$~m); Song et al.\ (2026) derive a Tube-MPC for
heavy-haul virtually-coupled train sets. It is therefore the natural baseline
and the strongest reference point.

Yet we argue that MPC carries \textbf{four structural limitations} in exactly
this regime, and that a \emph{model-free, global-horizon} cooperative policy
(multi-agent reinforcement learning, MARL) is the better fit:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Finite-horizon feasibility is fragile; terminal constraints are
hard to design.} MPC solves a local receding-horizon optimization. For
nonlinear coupled systems a control-invariant \emph{terminal set} must be
imposed for recursive feasibility, but designing one is notoriously difficult
\cite{derajic2025}; when the horizon is too short to reach it the
QP/NLP is infeasible, and lengthening the horizon raises computation and, under
non-convexity, degrades solver reliability \cite{surma2025}. In our
cyclic-braking platoon the relevant dynamics time-scale is \emph{minutes}, far
beyond a real-time-feasible horizon.
\item \textbf{It cannot reuse driving expertise / past experience.} A vanilla
MPC is an optimization over a model each step; it has no mechanism to ingest
driver braking practice or to improve from past runs. Learning must be bolted
on (LMPC \cite{vaquero2025}).
\item \textbf{It requires an extremely precise model.} SLSQP/MPC relies on an
accurate forward model of coupler forces, brake-wave propagation and
brake--release hysteresis --- exactly the components that are hardest to model
precisely. With model mismatch MPC has no buffer (our simplified MPC without a
coupler model overflowed the coupler limit).
\item \textbf{It does not generalize across trains and lines.} Each consist
differs (mass, length, brake characteristics), and MPC must be re-tuned and
re-identified per train and per section; it optimizes locally and does not
transfer learned structure across operating conditions.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Our thesis.} Model-free MARL is not a ``less rigorous'' alternative to
MPC; it is structurally better suited here: it (i)~learns a \emph{global
long-horizon} driving rhythm instead of a shrinking local window, (ii) can be
\emph{seeded/regularized with driver rules} (rule-based cyclic braking as a
safety-embedded inner layer), (iii) is \emph{model-free}, so it does not need a
perfect coupler/braking model, and (iv) \emph{generalizes} across randomized
initial conditions, larger platoons, and unseen profiles.

We demonstrate this on the real Shuozhou profile with 10{,}000-tonne trains and
a multi-train platoon. Under the \emph{same} randomized-initialization protocol
our hierarchical MARL policy (M-MARL) holds spacing and coupler force and never
collides (reward $+36{,}004$; $0$ collisions), whereas a \emph{fair}
multi-train MPC with the full model, coupler constraint, and gap constraints
\emph{collapses}: speed stalls below the release limit for $71.6\%$ of run time,
coupler force exceeds the limit ($1{,}178$--$1{,}431$~kN), and under randomized
initial conditions the spacing goes negative (rear-end collision, 330 collision
steps; reward $-258{,}831 \pm 116{,}023$). The collapse maps directly onto
MPC's short-rolling-window blind spot: its $\approx 3.2$~s horizon cannot
foresee the minute-level brake--release rhythm.

\textbf{Contributions.} (C1) We formalize the four structural limitations of
MPC for long-downhill cyclic-braking platoon control and map each to a MARL
advantage; (C2) we provide a controlled head-to-head (same environment, same
safety layer, randomized protocol) showing MPC's receding-horizon blind spot
empirically collapses the run while MARL transfers; (C3) we show MARL's
generalization (50 random initial conditions, 5-train platoons, a second 20-km
profile) that model-driven MPC cannot offer for free.

%-------------------------- II. RELATED WORK --------------------------
\section{Related Work}

\textbf{MPC is the industrial incumbent for train control and virtual
coupling.} Yang et al.\ (ITSC 2025) use single-train multi-body MPC with
cyclic air braking and coupler-impulse minimization on the Shuozhou profile;
Wang et al.\ (2026) extend improved MPC with Kalman filtering for heavy-haul
virtual coupling (gap error $\leq 20$~m); Song et al.\ (2026) construct a
Tube-MPC for heavy-haul virtually-coupled train sets with explicit
\emph{terminal controllers and terminal constraint sets} to certify stability;
Lu et al.\ (ICIRT 2025) use NMPC for multiple locomotives of a single train.
These confirm MPC is both the practical standard and an active research object.

\textbf{MPC's known theoretical fragility (Lim~1).} Stability and recursive
feasibility of MPC rest on a control-invariant terminal set; designing one is
``particularly difficult, especially in dynamic environments''
\cite{derajic2025}, and short horizons can make the NLP infeasible while
longer horizons raise cost and, under non-convexity, hurt solver reliability
\cite{surma2025}. Surveys of virtual-coupling control classify MPC's main
limitation as its finite-horizon/optimization burden relative to real-time
coupling requirements \cite{wu2023}.

\textbf{Learning is being grafted onto MPC (Lim~2 \& 3).} LMPC incorporates
data-driven learning of unmodeled dynamics into the optimization
\cite{vaquero2025}, acknowledging that plain MPC cannot learn from experience
and is sensitive to model error. Robust/learning variants are themselves active
research --- and the very need to add learning is evidence that the plain
method is being patched rather than intrinsically capable.

\textbf{Model-free / RL turn (our approach).} Roadmaps for RL-based virtual
coupling \cite{basile2022} and coordinated-operation MARL \cite{liu2025} show
growing evidence that model-free methods handle the constraint-satisfaction
complexity that model-driven local optimization finds hard. Our hierarchical
design (rule-based cyclic braking as a safety-embedded inner/control layer +
MARL for the cooperative outer layer) is the model-free alternative that
directly targets the four MPC limitations.

%-------------------------- III. PROBLEM & MODEL --------------------------
\section{Problem Statement and System Model}
\label{sec:model}

Heavy-haul platoon of $N$ trains, each a multi-body unit (1 locomotive +
$\approx 100$ wagons $\approx 10{,}000$~t) on a long downhill grade profile.
Longitudinal dynamics per vehicle: traction $-$ braking $-$ Davis resistance
$\pm$ grade $-$ coupler forces; air braking propagates along the consist at
finite wave speed ($\approx 200$~m/s $\rightarrow$ seconds of delay across a
$\approx 4$~km train). Coupler forces arise from a mass-spring-damper model
with a gap dead-zone. Control objective: keep each train speed in
$[v_{\min},v_{\max}]$, keep inter-train gap $\geq$ safety threshold, and keep
coupler force $\leq 1000$~kN, over a \emph{crossed-minutes} horizon. A short
linear MPC cannot see this horizon or model the switched coupler/braking force
exactly --- the crux of the comparison.

%-------------------------- IV. PROPOSED METHOD --------------------------
\section{Proposed Method}
\label{sec:method}

We retain the hierarchical structure but re-frame it explicitly as the
\emph{model-free} answer to the four MPC limitations.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Inner/control layer (model-based-rule, safety-embedded):}
rule-based cyclic braking (brake $> v_{high}$, release $< v_{low}$) plus a hard
coupler-force/ATP safety layer. This encodes driver expertise (addresses
Lim~2 by soft-seeding) and does \emph{not} need an explicit predicted model
(addresses Lim~3).
\item \textbf{Outer/cooperative layer (learned):} MAPPO, one agent per train,
observes its own state plus the leader's, and outputs a cooperative threshold
offset --- i.e., it learns the \emph{global, long-horizon} cooperative rhythm
that MPC's local window cannot represent (addresses Lim~1). The policy
generalizes across initial conditions, platoons and sections (addresses
Lim~4).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{MPC under randomized initial conditions (5 seeds):} reward
$-258{,}831 \pm 116{,}023$, coupler $1{,}431 \pm 207$~kN ($>$limit), gap min
$-9 \pm 94$~m, $329.6 \pm 384.2$ collision steps, only $4.2$ cyclic-braking
cycles, and $56.5$~ms/step solver time (vs.\ $\mu$s--ms for the learned
policy).

\textbf{Mapping to the four MPC limitations (core evidence):}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Lim~1 (receding-horizon blinkering):} the $\approx 3.2$~s window
stalls the train ($71.6\%$--$70.8\%$ below release floor) and collapses spacing
($-9$~m, 330 collisions). MARL's global policy holds 0 collisions.
\item \textbf{Lim~2/3 (model \& experience):} the \emph{fair} MPC already has
the full coupler model yet still overflows the coupler limit
($1{,}178$--$1{,}431$~kN); a simplified MPC (no coupler model) overflows more.
MARL needs no coupler predictor and is seeded with rule-based braking, staying
at $659$~kN.
\item \textbf{Lim~4 (generalization):} M-MARL keeps $+36{,}120$ / 0 collisions
on an out-of-domain $20$~km profile and scales to 5-train platoons; MPC is a
per-run local optimum with no transfer.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Pressure / scenario ablations (supporting):} the cooperative benefit
grows monotonically as the target gap shrinks ($+26{,}213 \rightarrow
+47{,}502$), consistent with the spacing-limited regime being exactly where the
model-free cooperative layer matters.

%-------------------------- VI. CONCLUSION --------------------------
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We re-frame heavy-haul cyclic-braking platoon control as a regime in which
MPC's four structural limitations --- finite-horizon/terminal feasibility,
inability to reuse driving experience, sensitivity to model error, and weak
generalization --- are intrinsic properties rather than tuning issues, and
where model-free hierarchical MARL addresses each. Controlled experiments on
the real Shuozhou profile show the fair MPC collapses under the minute-scale
cyclic-braking rhythm (stall, coupler-force overflow, rear-end collision)
while MARL holds spacing and coupler force and generalizes. \textbf{Limitations:}
MARL does not provide the formal recursive-stability guarantee that
tube/MPC methods can certify; for the nominal short-horizon case MPC remains
competitive; deployment safety would require formal verification/ATP
integration; and the RL statistical outlier (1/3 seeds of an earlier model)
must be reported honestly.
