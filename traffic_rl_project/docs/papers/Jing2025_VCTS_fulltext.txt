 
Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning Optimization for Virtual
Coupled Train Set Problem in CBTC Systems
Guanlin Jing, Yifei Zou*, Zuyuan Zhang, Jiguo Yu, Dongxiao Yu, and Xiuzhen Cheng
Abstract: Modern  urban  rail  transit  systems  face  unprecedented  challenges  and  opportunities  due  to
accelerated urbanization and growing travel demand, which increase the need for operational efficiency and
service  quality.  Traditional  approaches  based  on  Communication-Based  Train  Control  (CBTC)  systems  are
insufficient for fluctuating passenger flows. This study introduces a Virtual Coupled Train Set (VCTS) controlled
by  Multi-Agent  Reinforcement  Learning  (MARL)  to  address  these  issues.  VCTS  enhances  capacity  and
efficiency  by  dynamically  adjusting  train  spacing  and  formations  through  train-to-train  communication  and
onboard  sensors.  The  MARL  framework  optimizes  collaborative  control  in  high-dimensional  state  spaces,
complex action spaces, and dynamic environments. Simulation results demonstrate that the proposed MARL
algorithm significantly improves train operation efficiency and safety compared to both traditional methods and
previous reinforcement learning approaches. This research advances the application of the Internet of Things
in rail transit, offering innovative solutions for safer, more efficient, and intelligent urban rail systems.
Key words:  reinforcement  learning; Virtually  Coupled  Train  Sets  (VCTSs); Communication-Based  Train  Control
(CBTC)
1　Introduction
The  modern  urban  rail  transit  system  is  currently
experiencing both unique challenges and opportunities
due  to  factors  such  as  advances  in  technology,
increasing  demand  for  sustainable  transportation,  and
the need for infrastructure upgrades. On the one hand,
the accelerated process of urbanization and the growing
travel demand of residents have put higher demands on
the  operational  efficiency  and  service  quality  of  rail
transit;  on  the  other  hand,  the  development  and
application  of  emerging  technologies  provide  new
ideas and possibilities for solving these problems.

system[1],  which  realizes  automatic  driving  and
dispatching  of  trains  through  train-to-ground
communication  and  automatic  train  protection,
ensuring  driving  safety.  This  model  typically  uses  a
uniform  train  departure  interval,  i.e.,  trains  depart

distribution  of  passenger  flow  (such  as  during  peak
hours and sudden passenger flows during large events),
the  existing  train  operation  plans  are  increasingly

To  further  improve  the  operational  efficiency  and
service quality of the rail transit system, it is urgent to
introduce innovative operational organization methods
and  advanced  technical  means.  Among  them,  the
Virtual Coupled Train Set (VCTS)[3] is a new type of
train  formation  and  operation  control  method  that  can
better adapt to passenger flow fluctuations and improve
line  transportation  capacity  by  dynamically  adjusting
train spacing and flexibly changing train formations.
VCTS  uses  collaborative  control  between  trains
using  using  Train-to-Train  (T2T)  and  train-to-ground
communication along with onboard sensors. Instead of
physical  coupling,  VCTS  trains  use  reliable  wireless
tech  to  maintain  an “electronic  coupling”,  allowing
dynamic  adjustment  of  train  spacing  based  on
operational  needs.  Combined  with  onboard  lidar,
millimeter-wave  radar,  vision  sensors,  and  other
equipment,  VCTS  can  support  high  automation  and
intelligence  of  train  operations  while  ensuring  driving
safety.
Trains of VCTS can achieve autonomous recoupling
and  decoupling  operations  based  on  transport  demand
and  planning  T2T  wireless  communication[4].  In  the
VCTS  system  based  on  the  Industrial  Internet  of
Things  (IIoT),  to  successfully  achieve  the  control
objectives  of  headway  and  speed,  all  other  units  must
be  coordinated  through  the  Train  Cooperation
Controller  (TCO),  where  the  TCO,  as  the  core  of  the
control  strategy,  is  responsible  for  maintaining  the
uniform  linear  formation  of  the  leading  train  and  the
continuous  operation  path  of  the  overall  train  by
adjusting  the  speed  and  headway  of  the  following
trains[5].  Furthermore,  in  such  a  VCTS  system,  the
concept  of  braking  distance  has  also  changed  from
absolute  braking  distance  to  relative  braking  distance.
This  shift  means  that  the  implementation  of  braking
measures  is  more  reliant  on  the  relative  position
between trains and the dynamic environment[6].
Such an urban rail transit system based on VCTS can
significantly  enhance  line  capacity  and  operational
efficiency,  precisely  match  passenger  flow  with
transport  capacity,  and  provide  passengers  with  more
comfortable  and  convenient  travel  services.  At  the
same  time,  as  a  new  train  operation  control  method,
VCTS  also  puts  higher  demands  on  the  safety,
reliability,  and  intelligence  level  of  rail  transit,
necessitating  systematic  research  and  exploration  in
key  technology  development,  testing,  validation,  and
standardization. Therefore, the member trains of VCTS
have  a  certain  degree  of  autonomy  and  require  strict
timeliness  and  reliability  in  wireless  communication.
However, in the VCTS scenario, the complexity of the
communication network and the high-speed mobility of
trains  pose  severe  challenges  to  achieving  optimal
collaborative  control.  Traditional  strategies  cannot
meet  the  performance  requirements  of  VCTS.  Multi-
Agent  Reinforcement  Learning  (MARL)[7−11] allows
trains  to  intelligently  explore  optimal  solutions[12] in
this  complex  environment.  Thus,  reinforcement
learning control has become an emerging technology in
optimizing  VCTS  collaborative  control  within  CBTC
systems. Specifically, the new generation of IIoT[13, 14]
connects a large number of user devices and sensors to
local  units  (such  as  edge  devices  in  edge  computing)
with  some  basic  storage  and  computing  resources
through  high-speed  5th/6th  Generation  Mobile
Communication  Technology  (5G/6G)  networks.
Therefore,  a  series  of  VCTS  applications  can  be
deployed  on  train  carriages  close  to  data  resources,
providing users with real-time and intelligent services.
Fraga-Lamas[15] proposed  using  IIoT  to  improve
railway  safety  levels.  IIoT  is  also  used  to  enhance
railway,  including  track  and  facility  maintenance
capabilities[5].  With  the  integration  of  IIoT,  big  data,
and  artificial  intelligence,  railway  maintenance
becomes  more  efficient  and  intelligent,  enabling
condition-based  maintenance[16].  Similarly,  trains  can
move  synchronously  like  VCTS,  meaning  multiple
trains  can  be  regarded  as  a  fleet,  thereby  increasing
train capacity.
However, there is little research on the performance
evaluation and analysis of IIoT-based VCTS, which is
urgently  needed  to  support  the  application  of  IIoT  in
Urban  Rail  Transit  (URT).  Due  to  the  dynamic
characteristics  of  trains  and  the  critical  requirements
for  T2T  communication,  achieving  VCTS  consistency
and  coverage  control  is  challenging.  The  number  of
IIoT  states,  including  numerous  train  devices  and
sensing  nodes,  will  be  so  large  that  no  centralized
machine  learning  algorithm  can  be  executed.  In

2　Related Work
A typical CBTC system is essential for modern urban
rail  transit,  ensuring  safe  and  efficient  operations
through its structured components[1, 17, 18]. At the heart
of  the  system  is  the  ground  control  center[19],  which
includes  key  elements  such  as  Automatic  Train
Supervision  (ATS),  Zone  Controller  (ZC),  and
Computer  Interlocking  (CI).  These  components  work
together  to  oversee  and  manage  the  overall  system
operations.  The  Data  Storage  Unit  (DSU)  within  the
ground  control  center  is  responsible  for  storing  all
necessary  data,  facilitating  continuous  and  reliable
system  performance.  The  train  subsystem  plays  a
critical  role  by  collecting  various  line  data  and
generating  operation  commands  based  on  instructions
received  from  the  ground  control  center.  This
subsystem  is  equipped  with  the  Vehicle  On-Board

Controller (VOBC), which contains both the Automatic
Train Protection (ATP) and Automatic Train Operation
(ATO)  modules.  These  modules  ensure  that  trains
operate  within  safe  speed  limits  and  follow  the
Movement Authority (MA) directives generated by the
ZC.
Communication  between  the  ground  control  center
and the train subsystem is maintained through the Data
Communication  System  (DCS).  The  DCS  includes
Base Band Units (BBU), Remote Radio Units (RRU),
and  a  backbone  network,  which  together  establish
robust  communication  links[20].  Trains  continuously

information,  alongside  track  occupancy  data  provided
by track-side equipment and routes arranged by the CI,
to calculate and issue MAs[21].
The  MA  determines  the  safe  distance  to  the  nearest
obstacle,  including  other  trains  or  track  switches,
ensuring  safe  train  operations.  High-precision  control
algorithms  enhance  the  efficiency  and  safety  of  the
system  by  providing  accurate  MAs.  The  ATO
subsystem[22] on  the  train  uses  the  MA  to  generate
commands  for  accelerating,  cruising,  or  braking,
thereby maintaining optimal train performance.
2.1　IIoT CBTC-based VCTS
Sudden surges in passenger flow, such as those caused
by  concerts,  sports  matches,  and  holidays,  present  a
significant  challenge  for  URT  systems.  These  events
can  create  unexpected  spikes  in  demand,  requiring
more  trains  in  one  direction  while  the  opposite
direction needs fewer trains[23].
VCTS  is  designed  to  address  this  issue.  Unlike
traditional  CBTC  systems,  where  trains  run  at  fixed
intervals,  VCTS  allows  trains  to  operate  closer
together, forming a train platoon. This setup increases
capacity as trains can maintain smaller intervals among
one another[24]. In a VCTS, trains in the high-demand
direction  stop  at  each  station  to  pick  up  passengers,
while in the low-demand direction, only the first train
in  the  platoon  makes  stops,  thus  optimizing  train  use
and reducing listed capacity[23].
The IIoT enhances CBTC systems by enabling real-
time  data  collection  and  analysis.  IIoT  devices  gather
information from train and track components, which is
processed  using  advanced  analytics  and  machine
learning to predict and prevent issues[25].
Safety  and  reliability  also  improve  with  IIoT.  Real-
time  data  allows  for  quick  and  accurate  decision-
making.  Additionally,  IIoT  helps  optimize  energy  use
by  adjusting  train  operations  based  on  current  track
conditions  and  traffic  flow,  leading  to  significant
energy savings[26]. In a VCTS, mechanical couplers are
replaced with wireless communication, enabling trains
to  operate  as  a  cohesive  unit.  The  system  leverages
real-time  data  exchange  to  maintain  safe  distances
between  trains,  thereby  enhancing  line  capacity  and
operational  flexibility[27].  For  both  URT  and  High-
Speed  Rail  (HSR),  VCTS  can  handle  emergency
situations  and  sudden  passenger  surges.  The  proposed
model takes into account the effects of high-speed train
movements  and  potential  interferences,  making  it
adaptable  for  both  types  of  rail  systems.  Thus,  with
appropriate  configurations,  VCTS  can  significantly
enhance rail control systems for various scenarios[28].
2.2　MARL for VCTS
Several studies have extensively examined the efficacy
of  real-time  learning  through  environmental  data
acquisition[8, 10, 12, 29, 30].  This  method  has  proven  to
enhance the performance of algorithms, particularly in
the  context  of  MARL.  In  recent  years,  MARL  has
gained  significant  interest  due  to  its  innovative
approach  to  solving  complex  problems.  MARL
involves  scenarios  where  two  or  more  independent
agents  interact  with  the  environment  and  each  other.
These  agents’ actions  are  influenced  by  their
surroundings, and they may exhibit varying degrees of
cooperation or competition. Consequently, MARL has
found  applications  in  diverse  areas  such  as  gaming,
robotics, communication, and transportation systems.
One promising application of MARL is in optimizing
VCTS.  In  a  VCTS  system,  each  train  unit  acts  as  an
independent agent within an MARL framework. These
agents  (train  units)  interact  with  the  shared
environment (railway network) and with each other to
optimize  their  collective  performance[5].  For  instance,
they  need  to  synchronize  speeds,  acceleration,  and
braking  to  maintain  optimal  distances,  ensure  safety,
and  minimize  energy  consumption.  In  multi-agent
scenarios, agents face numerous choices for their next
actions.  If  we  consider  all  agents  and  their  potential
actions  as  a  single  entity,  the  size  of  this  space
increases  exponentially  with  the  number  of  agents.
However,  an  extensive  action  space  can  hinder  the
algorithm’s resolution process, affecting the scalability
of the agent count[31].

3　System Model and Problem Formulation
We introduce the model of the VCTS and the problems
that  it  confronts.  Firstly,  we  provide  a  simplistic
scenario  description,  followed  by  an  elaborate
mathematical  model  of  the  VCTS  in  Section  3.1,
including  the  train  dynamics  model  and  artificial
potential  field  functions.  In  Section  3.2,  the  core
problem that this research aims to solve is outlined.
A  VCTS  consists  of  a  group  of  trains  that  operate
according  to  a  preordained  plan,  with  the  stipulation
that each train must halt at every station. The schematic
representation  of  the  concept  is  illustrated  in  the
corresponding Fig.  1.  Therefore,  the  operational
objectives of a VCTS are subject to temporal changes.
In  the  broader  sense,  a  VCTS  can  be  perceived  as  a
multi-agent system, and its operations can be modeled
as  a  motion  planning  problem.  However,  due  to  the
complex  and  dynamic  operational  conditions,
achieving  global  information  awareness  and  swiftly
arriving at optimal decisions is challenging.

3.2　Problem formulation
The goal of VCTS is to stabilize the distance between
adjacent  trains  and  converge  each  train’s  speed  over
time.  This  implies  that  VCTS  needs  to  regulate  both
train  speed  and  acceleration  to  maintain  a  constant

synchronize the speeds of all trains.

A key innovation of HHG-MAPPO is the integration
of  a  centralized  heuristic  optimal  state  generator .
This component leverages domain expertise to provide
guidance,  especially  in  the  early  stages  of  learning.
This  means  that  the  combination  of  conventional
random  selection  and  experience  has  effectively
reduced  and  compressed  the  state  space,  minimizing
the  duration  of  the  episode  without  compromising  the
accuracy  of  the  train’s  operational  status.  The
algorithm  probabilistically  chooses  between  acting
based on the current state or the heuristically generated
optimal state, with the probability of using the heuristic
guidance  decreasing  over  time.  The  training  process
involves  iterative  episodes  where  the  agent  interacts
with  the  VCTS  environment.  In  each  time  step,  the
agent selects an action, observes the resulting state and
reward, and stores this experience in a memory buffer.
The  algorithm  uses  these  collected  experiences  to
update  the  policy  and  value  networks.  The  policy
update  process  in  HHG-MAPPO  is  particularly
sophisticated.  It  uses  the  PPO  objective,  which  helps
maintain stable learning by limiting the extent of policy
changes in each update. Additionally, an auxiliary loss
term  is  introduced  to  encourage  the  value  function  to
align  with  the  heuristic  guidance.  This  auxiliary  loss
helps  accelerate  learning  by  leveraging  the  heuristic
knowledge.
To  balance  exploration  and  exploitation,  the
algorithm  incorporates  an  entropy  term  in  its  loss
function. This encourages the policy to maintain some
level  of  randomness,  preventing  premature
convergence  to  suboptimal  solutions.  Throughout
training, the algorithm gradually reduces its reliance on
heuristic guidance. This is achieved by decreasing both
the  probability  of  using  the  heuristic  state  for  action
selection  and  the  weight  of  the  auxiliary  loss.  This
adaptive  approach  allows  the  agent  to  benefit  from
expert knowledge early in training while progressively
relying  more  on  learned  behavior,  potentially
surpassing the performance of the heuristic alone.

5　Implementation
The  objective  of  VCTS  is  to  increase  transportation
efficiency and accommodate passengers. Although the
MARL-based controller strives to reduce the disparity
between  the  states  of  the  lead  and  subsequent  trains,
the lead train operates at a high velocity. The intention
of  cooperative  control  is  to  ensure  that  the  lead  train
halts  at  the  appropriate  location  with  a  safe  level  of
acceleration,  provides  passenger  comfort  during
smoother sections of the journey, and has the ability to
evade  or  stop  abruptly  in  unexpected  circumstances.

could  be  disrupted.  Therefore,  in  this  section,  the
efficacy of the HHG-MAPPO in the VCTS is evaluated
by  devising  three  different  scenarios.  Further  details
regarding  the  specific  environmental  setup  and  the
results  of  the  experiment  will  be  presented  in  the
succeeding sections.

6　Conclusion
This study optimizes co-control strategies for VCTS in
urban rail transit using MARL. Advanced technologies
boost efficiency and safety to tackle urbanization. We
utilize  innovative  VCTS  systems,  which  bolster  co-
control between trains by facilitating efficient vehicle-
to-vehicle interaction. The study uses MARL, ensuring
effective  coordination  amid  multiple  agents.  We
validate our MARL algorithm via simulations, showing
its  superiority  over  traditional  methods.  We  plan  to
fine-tune  these  algorithms  and  test  them  in  real  urban
rail  systems.  Despite  the  promising  results  obtained
using  MARL  for  optimizing  co-control  strategies  in
VCTS  for  urban  rail  transit,  challenges  remain.  One
limitation  is  the  scalability  of  the  MARL  algorithm
when  applied  to  more  complex  and  larger-scale
networks.  Furthermore,  our  algorithm’s  reliance  on
high-quality,  real-time  data  may  restrict  its  use  in
environments  where  such  data  inputs  are  inconsistent
or  unavailable.  In  future  work,  we  will  expand  this
approach  to  other  IIoT  applications  like  autonomous
driving,  greatly  contributing  to  urban  mobility  and
improving passenger experience.
