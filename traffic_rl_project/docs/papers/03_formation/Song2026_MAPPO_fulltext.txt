Abstract: To explore novel methodologies for enhancing the eﬀiciency of heavy-haul railway
transport systems, this paper investigates the intelligent cooperative control problem of
multiple independent heavy-haul trains forming an operational group. The core challenge lies
in achieving speed synchronization, distance maintenance, and overall energy eﬀiciency
optimization of multiple trains at the macroscopic operation level under strict safety
constraints. Addressing the high-dimensionality, nonlinearity, and distributed decision-making
characteristics of this problem, this paper proposes a fully data-driven multi-agent
reinforcement learning framework. Abandoning reliance on precise analytical models, each
train is modeled as an agent that autonomously learns cooperative control strategies through
continuous trial-and-error interaction with the environment. This paper first establishes a
detailed longitudinal dynamics model of heavy-haul trains as a high-fidelity simulation
environment for agent interaction. Furthermore, a training framework is designed centered on
the Multi-Agent Proximal Policy Optimization algorithm, supplemented by
potential-field-based safety guidance rewards and multi-objective optimization rewards.
Simulation experiments demonstrate that compared with the standard Multi-Agent Proximal
Policy Optimization baseline, the proposed data-driven framework can significantly improve
the speed synchronization accuracy of the group and reduce overall traction energy
consumption while ensuring zero safety violations.
Key words: heavy-haul train; train group cooperative control; data-driven control

Introduction
Heavy-haul railway transportation serves as the core carrier within the bulk cargo lo-
gistics system, where its transportation eﬀiciency is directly correlated with the stability of
energy and raw material supply chains [1]. To further exploit line capacity and reduce opera-
tional costs, the operational mode of heavy-haul trains is progressively evolving from traditional
single-train operations toward Virtual Coupling and automatic driving technologies based on
Vehicle-to-Vehicle (V2V) communication [2].
However, a heavy-haul train is intrinsically a
high-dimensional multi-mass system characterized by significant time delays and strong non-
linearities[3], subject to constraints imposed by locomotive traction forces, complex nonlinear
coupler forces, and air braking systems with pronounced hysteresis characteristics [1, 4]. In
high-density Virtual Coupling scenarios, a fundamental contradiction exists between the re-
sponse lag of actuators and the demands for high-frequency cooperative control. If control
strategies fail to adequately account for the aforementioned dynamic constraints, severe safety
incidents—such as longitudinal impulses, coupler breakage, or even derailment—may easily

occur [1].
Faced with these complex dynamic challenges, existing industrial control strategies ex-
hibit distinct limitations in the cooperative control tasks of heavy-haul train groups. Although
Proportional-Integral-Derivative (PID) control dominated early single-locomotive control due
to its structural simplicity[5], its theoretical performance ceiling remains insuﬀicient when sat-
isfying the requirements of multi-agent cooperation within strongly nonlinear environments [6].
PID controllers rely on fixed gain parameters tuned for specific operating conditions, lacking the
adaptive capacity to handle system parameter perturbations[7]; consequently, they struggle to
cope with dynamic uncertainties introduced by variations in load status, marshaling length, and
line gradients [6]. More critically, PID is fundamentally a feedback control mechanism based on
current error, lacking feedforward compensation capabilities for the system’s large time delays.
For instance, when dealing with signal propagation delays in air braking systems that can last
tens of seconds, PID control logic fails to effectively suppress longitudinal impulses during the
braking wave propagation, easily triggering chain collisions and stretching between vehicles,
which causes coupler stress to exceed safety thresholds [6]. To address multi-variable coupling
and hard constraint issues, Model Predictive Control (MPC) has become a mainstream research
focus in academia in recent years. However, in the process of practical engineering translation,
MPC faces two major challenges: computational complexity and model mismatch [8]. On one
hand, as an ultra-high-dimensional system, the state space complexity of a heavy-haul train
grows rapidly with the number of vehicles; a train comprising 200 wagons involves over 400
state variables. Solving such large-scale, non-convex, and nonlinear optimization problems in
real-time on computationally constrained onboard embedded platforms is extremely diﬀicult,
making it hard to meet millisecond-level real-time control requirements [8]. On the other hand,
the control precision of MPC depends on the accuracy of the internal prediction model. To
reduce computational load, existing MPC schemes often employ simplified multi-mass models
or linearize the system, ignoring non-smooth gap characteristics between couplers and com-
plex fluid dynamic details [8].
This structural deviation between the simplified model and
the physical system leads to significant prediction errors during critical condition transitions,
and in extreme environments, it may even generate erroneous instructions that compromise
operational safety [8].
Given the real-time performance and applicability defects of model-based methods, Multi-
Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) offers a novel data-driven paradigm for resolving this
problem [9]. The spatial distribution and local interaction characteristics of heavy-haul train
groups naturally align with the “Centralized Training, Decentralized Execution” (CTDE) ar-
chitecture of MARL. Unlike MPC, which relies on high-precision models, MARL possesses
model-free characteristics, enabling it to directly extract nonlinear features such as coupler
gaps and braking hysteresis through interaction with the environment, thereby circumvent-

ing biases introduced by modeling simplification.
Furthermore, the CTDE paradigm shifts
computation-intensive policy optimization to the offline phase, while online control requires
only lightweight neural network inference. This compresses instruction generation time to the
millisecond level, significantly enhancing the system’s responsiveness to transient disturbances.
Despite the theoretical advantages of MARL, its practical deployment remains confronted
by three primary challenges [10]. First is the issue of safety assurance: classic RL relies on the
“soft constraints” of reward functions, which cannot mathematically guarantee hard physical
constraints such as coupler forces. In exploration phases or Out-of-Distribution (OOD) scenar-
ios, this can easily lead to prohibited actions, violating the “Fail-Safe” criterion of industrial
control [11].
Second is the Sim-to-Real gap: existing training environments are often con-
structed based on simplified dynamics[12], making it diﬀicult to reproduce complex wheel-rail
contact and time-varying resistance found on real lines, resulting in significant performance
degradation or instability of policy models in physical environments [10].
Finally, there is
the bias in communication environment assumptions: mainstream algorithms often presuppose
ideal communication, ignoring inevitable delays and packet losses in complex electromagnetic
environments like mountains or tunnels[12, 13]. Research indicates that cooperative decision-
making based on outdated information can trigger longitudinal oscillations in train groups,
severely undermining cooperative stability [14].
Addressing the aforementioned issues, this paper proposes a cooperative control enhance-
ment framework for heavy-haul train groups that integrates the mechanism of Artificial Poten-
tial Fields (APF). The main contributions of this paper are as follows:
1. The cooperative control problem of heavy-haul train groups is modeled as a Multi-Agent
Reinforcement Learning task, utilizing the CTDE paradigm to achieve model-free, dis-
tributed real-time cooperative control without the need for precise analytical models.
2. Artificial Potential Field theory is introduced to optimize the reward mechanism, re-
constructing discrete safety constraints such as collision avoidance and coupler breakage
prevention into continuous, differentiable potential energy penalties, thereby providing
agents with explicit gradient information for risk avoidance.
3. A high-fidelity simulation platform containing complex longitudinal dynamic characteris-
tics is constructed, verifying the effectiveness of the introduced potential field mechanism
in improving algorithm convergence speed and ensuring operational safety.
The remainder of this paper is organized as follows: Section 1 elaborates on the system
modeling of heavy-haul train groups; Section 2 introduces the proposed Multi-Agent Reinforce-
ment Learning method enhanced by potential fields; Section 3 presents experimental results
and analysis; and Section 4 concludes the paper.

System Modeling
Constructing a high-fidelity simulation interactive environment and a rational group infor-
mation topology serves as the foundation for researching the cooperative control of heavy-haul
train groups.
This chapter aims to establish a system model oriented towards data-driven
control from two dimensions: physical evolution mechanisms and information interaction ar-
chitecture. Specifically, by introducing nonlinear coupler characteristics and complex line re-
sistances, a multi-point mass longitudinal dynamics model is constructed to accurately capture
the longitudinal impulse and energy transfer processes under long formation conditions. Fur-
thermore, combined with the actual operation conditions of heavy-haul railways, the commu-
nication topology of the train group is established, and the local observation space and action
space of the agents are defined, thereby completing the mapping from physical entities to the
multi-agent reinforcement learning environment.
1.1

research, this section establishes a longitudinal dynamics model based on a multi-point mass
system. This approach allows for the accurate capture of critical heavy-haul characteristics,
including long formations, large inertia, and complex transmission of coupler forces [15]. Specif-

a multi-body system connected by nonlinear spring-damper units representing coupler connec-
tions[16]. This model accurately simulates internal longitudinal impulses and energy transfer
processes, thereby providing a high-fidelity interactive environment for subsequent data-driven
algorithms.

1.2
Group Communication Topology and State Space
The heavy-haul train group consists of N homogeneous or heterogeneous heavy-haul train
units. To ensure system robustness and minimize communication bandwidth usage, this study
adopts the Predecessor-Following combined with Global Broadcast communication topology,
which is widely recognized in engineering practice for its maturity and reliability [17]. Within
this topology, the i-th train (i = 2, . . . , N) acquires real-time running state information of
its immediate predecessor i −1 via Vehicle-to-Vehicle (V2V) communication links [18], while
simultaneously receiving the global reference command vref(t) from the ground dispatch center
via the Train-to-Ground wireless network. As the group leader, the first train (i = 1), having
no physical predecessor, makes decisions primarily based on global reference commands and
line speed limit information, serving as the benchmark to guide the subsequent trains.

2
Data-Driven Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning
Framework
Addressing the inherent strong nonlinear coupling and multi-objective conflicts in the coop-
erative control of heavy-haul train groups, traditional control methods based on analytical mod-
els often fail to balance precision [19], real-time performance, and generalization capabilities.
Therefore, this chapter constructs a fully data-driven MARL control framework, transforming
the cooperative cruising problem into a sequential decision-making process within a complex
dynamic environment. This framework is centered on the MAPPO algorithm and adopts a
CTDE architecture to adapt to actual train communication constraints. By designing a com-
posite reward mechanism that integrates potential field safety guidance and energy eﬀiciency
optimization, it solves the exploration dilemma under sparse rewards. Furthermore, relying
on a high-fidelity simulation environment for closed-loop iteration, the framework achieves
autonomous evolution and robustness improvement of cooperative strategies.
2.1
Fundamentals of the MAPPO Algorithm
Given the characteristics of continuous action spaces, high state dimensions, and dynamic
environmental changes in heavy-haul train group cooperative control tasks, this paper selects
the MAPPO algorithm [20, 21], which has demonstrated superior performance and robustness
in multi-agent cooperative tasks. The framework adopts a CTDE architecture, aiming to solve
the non-stationarity problem caused by the simultaneous evolution of agent strategies in multi-
agent environments, while simultaneously satisfying the requirements of actual train operations
for distributed real-time decision-making.

2.1.1
Algorithm Principles
Under this architecture, each heavy-haul train is treated as an independent agent, and the
system comprises two core neural network components working in tandem. The Distributed
Execution Policy Network, denoted as πθi, operates during the inference or actual operation
phase.
It receives only the agent’s local observation vector ot
i and maps this input to the
probability distribution parameters of the action via a Multi-Layer Perceptron. Subsequently,
it samples to obtain the continuous traction or braking control command ut
i.
This design
ensures that the train can independently complete cooperative driving tasks relying solely on
Vehicle-to-Vehicle communication and local perception.
Conversely, the Centralized Training Value Network, denoted as Vϕ, is introduced during
the training phase to assist the Actor in learning the global optimal policy. This network has
access to global state information st, including observation information of all trains, global
environmental parameters, and unobserved hidden states. The Critic network calculates the
Generalized Advantage Estimation by evaluating the value of the current joint state, thereby
providing low-variance guidance signals for the gradient updates of each Actor network. No-
tably, after training is completed, the Critic network is detached and does not participate in
online control.

2.2
Reward Function Design
In reinforcement learning frameworks that do not rely on precise analytical models, the
design of the reward function dictates the direction of agent policy iteration and the perfor-
mance boundaries of final convergence. Addressing the multi-objective optimization challenges
faced in the cooperative control of heavy-haul train, specifically the need to simultaneously
achieve high-precision speed tracking, compact formation maintenance, and minimal energy
consumption under the premise of strictly guaranteeing operational safety, a single reward sig-
nal is insuﬀicient. Therefore, this paper designs a multi-dimensional composite reward function
mechanism, transforming sparse terminal task objectives into dense single-step guidance sig-
nals. The total reward rt

terms: cooperative tracking, safety potential field guidance, and operational energy eﬀiciency.

The experimental scenario simulates a cooperative formation consisting of N = 3 homoge-
neous heavy-haul trains, denoted as Train 1, Train 2, and Train 3. Unlike simplified single-mass
models, this study adopts the multi-point mass longitudinal dynamics model established in Sec-
tion 1 to accurately capture internal force transmission. Specifically, each train unit consists
of one locomotive and 19 heavy-duty wagons. The mass of the locomotive is 150, 000 kg, and
each wagon weighs 80, 000 kg, resulting in a total single train mass of 1, 670 t. Regarding dy-
namics, the connections between vehicles are modeled using nonlinear spring-damper elements
to simulate hysteresis effects and slack characteristics.
To ensure formation stability and decouple error propagation, a Virtual Leader strategy
within a Predecessor-Following topology is employed. Train 1 acts as the leader and tracks
a Virtual Reference Point moving strictly according to the global schedule and optimal speed
profile vref(t) to prevent oscillation amplification at the head of the formation. Meanwhile,
Train 2 and Train 3 operate in follower mode, tracking the state of their immediate predecessor
via V2V communication while simultaneously referencing the global guidance.
The simulation covers a complete operation cycle to test the dynamic adaptability of the
MARL agents, encompassing starting, acceleration, cruising, and braking phases. Throughout
these phases, the control objectives are set to minimize the speed tracking error and distance
error while strictly ensuring that the maximum internal coupler force remains within the safety

Conclusion
This paper deeply investigated the topic of heavy-haul train group cooperative control,
which holds significant engineering importance.
Addressing the dependence of traditional
model-driven methods on precise mathematical models and their limitations, this paper pio-
neered and implemented a fully data-driven multi-agent reinforcement learning solution frame-
work. The main work and conclusions include:
1. Established a highly engineered heavy-haul train multi-point mass longitudinal dynamics
simulation model, providing a realistic training and testing environment for data-driven
algorithms.
- 17 -
10.61951/SciencepaperOnline.202605.0114

˖ڍመ੾᝶஠ڙጲ
http://www.paper.edu.cn
2. Designed a purely data-driven learning framework centered on MAPPO, integrating coop-
erative tracking, potential field safety guidance, and multi-objective economic optimiza-
tion.
3. Through systematic simulation experiments and comparative analysis, verified that the
comprehensive performance of the proposed framework in ensuring operational safety,
improving speed synchronization accuracy, reducing overall energy consumption, and im-
proving running smoothness is significantly superior to traditional methods, and demon-
strates excellent adaptive capabilities and robustness.
This study successfully demonstrates the immense potential of data-driven methods in com-
plex industrial cooperative control problems, providing a new technical path for the intelligent
upgrade of heavy-haul railway operations.
