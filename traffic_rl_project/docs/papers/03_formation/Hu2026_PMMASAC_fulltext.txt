Abstract 
Virtual coupling control of trains is a promising technology for improving railway capac-
ity and operational efficiency. However, existing multi -agent reinforcement learning 
(MARL) approaches struggle to capture long -sequence temporal dependencies among 
train states in complex multi -train interaction scenarios, resulting in limited robustness 
and coordination stability. To address this issue, this paper proposes a Predictive Mamba-
based Multi -Agent Soft Actor –Critic (PM -MASAC) framework. A Mamba -based state 
prediction module is embedded into  the centralized Critic network to model historical 
state sequences and generate predictive state representations, thereby enhancing value 
estimation accuracy. In addition, a multi -agent aggregated prioritized experience replay  
(PER) mechanism is introduced to improve the utilization of critical cooperative samples 
and stabilize training. A hierarchical local –global reward structure is further designed to 
ensure individual tracking performance while promoting overall formation coordination. 
Experimental results under realistic railway operating conditions demonstrate that PM -
MASAC achieves superior robustness compared with baseline MARL methods. Velocity 
and spacing tracking errors are maintained within 3% and 1%, respectively, and the 
steady-state formation success rate exceeds 95.7% in the training environment. 
Keywords: virtual coupling; multi-agent soft actor-critic; predictive Mamba block;  
prioritized experience replay mechanism 
 
1. Introduction 
With the rapid growth of railway transportation demand, further improving 
transport efficiency and reducing energy consumption while ensuring operational safety 
has become a central research issue in modern railway systems. Traditional train opera-
tion control technologies are primarily based on  the train schedule and movable block 
mechanism. Although these approaches satisfy existing transportation requirements to a 
certain extent, their operational efficiency is gradually approaching theoretical limits.  
In recent years, the Vehicle-to-Vehicle Communication Based Train Control  (VBTC) 
[1] system enables information sharing and cooperative decision -making among trains 
through vehicle-to-vehicle communication. Multiple trains can operate cooperatively at 
reduced headways without physical coupling and can dynamically couple and decouple

study virtual coupling,  demonstrating that it can effectively increase line capacity, with 
passenger throughput improved from 15,000 to 23,000 per hour per direction. Tian et al. 
[4] analyzed energy consumption in virtual coupling systems under different control 
modes, demonstrating a strong correlation between train operating intervals, control 
strategies, and energy efficiency. Fadhel et al. [5] investigated the aerodynamic benefits of 
virtual coupling using CFD (Computational Fluid Dynamics) simulations, showing that 
close train spacing can significantly reduce drag and improve energy efficiency. However, 
in dynamic and complex operational environments, achieving coop erative control and

Existing research on virtual coupling control of trains predominantly adopts model-
based control methods, such as proportional –integral–derivative (PID) control [6], linear 
quadratic regulator (LQR) [7], model predictive control (MPC) [8], and sliding mode con-
trol (SMC) [9] . Liu et al. [10] propose a tube-based model predictive control (MPC) ap-
proach and present simulation results that verify its stronger robustness and higher effi-
ciency in maintaining asymptotic and string stability under disturbances and delays. He 
et al. [11] proposed an adaptive sliding mode controller for virtual coupling train control, 
enabling safe close -distance tracking while improving tracking performance and trans-
portation efficiency. Luo et al. [12] employed a PID -based approach that integrates feed-
forward and PID controllers to achieve accurate tracking and stable formation control. Du 
et al. [13] developed a virtual coupling train group controller based on ILQR (Iterative 
Linear Quadratic Regulator), integrating a dynamic train model to achieve stable for-
mation control. Felez et al. [14] presents a robust model predictive control scheme for vir-
tual coupling in railways, which introduces two types of uncertainties to enhance opera-
tional safety while maintaining service efficiency. These studies have achieved progress 
in reducing headways and improving tracking accuracy. However, such approaches typ-
ically rely on fixed control rules or accurate dynamic models, making it difficult to char-
acterize the complex and time -varying operating conditions in virtual coupling systems. 
As a result, the robustness and stability remain limited. 
In recent years, Artificial Intelligence (AI) technologies [15] have provided new solu-
tions for complex transportation system control. Reinforcement Learning (RL)  [16], as an 
important branch of AI, addresses sequential decision -making problems under the Mar-
kov Decision Process (MDP) framework  [17]. Due to its strong adaptability in nonlinear 
and strongly coupled systems, RL has been applied to train energy -saving control [18], 
autonomous driving [19], and traffic scheduling  [20]. In virtual coupling scenarios, deep 
RL methods based on the actor–critic architecture can achieve cooperative control through 
interaction learning. Lang et al. [21] proposed a speed profile optimization method based 
on DQN (Deep Q-Network) for virtual coupling trains, which improves train synchroni-
zation and punctuality. Basile et al.  [22] applied the DDPG (Deep Deterministic Policy 
Gradient) reinforcement learning algorithm to the virtual coupling control of automated 
train platoons, successfully achieving formation control. Chen et al.  [23] combined the 
SAC algorithm with a traditional PID controller, effectively improving tracking accuracy 
and formation stability. Liao et al. [24] applied deep reinforcement learning (DRL) to train 
timetable rescheduling in metro systems, demonstrating strong learning efficiency and 
optimization capability. Mao et al. [25] designed a dual-layer deep reinforcement learning 
(DL-DRL) method for large -scale multi-UAV (Unmanned Aerial Vehicles) task schedul-

the typical long -sequence, strongly coupled multi -agent cooperative control scenario of 
train virtual coupling, the variations in speed, position, and acceleration among trains ex-
hibit significant temporal correlation s. Decisions based only on current observations fail

To address the above limitation, researchers have incorporated deep sequential mod-
els with temporal modeling capabilities, such as LSTM  (Long Short-Term Memory) and 
Transformer, into RL frameworks to better exploit historical information and enhance fu-
ture state prediction. Karimzadeh et al. [26] integrated an LSTM-based prediction frame-

gers, improving prediction accuracy. Tseng et al. [27] proposed an LSTM-based predictive

model to enhance reinforcement learning policy training through self -supervised objec-
tives, significantly improving sample efficiency in continuous control tasks. Liu et al. [29] 
proposed the Sc ene-Rep Transformer framework, which improves SAC -based autono-
mous driving decision-making in dense traffic scenarios by exploring latent information 
from sequence prediction.  Although these methods have shown effectiveness in traffic 
prediction and autonomous driving, LSTM is limited by its recursive structure and suffers 
from gradient descent when modeling long sequence in multi-train cooperative decision-
making tasks, while Transformer exhibits powerful global modeling capabilities, the lack 
of explicit state -space inductive bias can make it less suitable for modeling cont inuous 
dynamical systems. 
Recently, the Mamba model, based on the Selective State Space Model (SSM), has 
emerged as a promising architecture for sequential modeling. With linear computational 
complexity and efficient long-sequence modeling capability, Mamba demonstrates signif-
icant advantages in temporal data processing. Several studies have incorporated Mamba 
into RL frameworks to enhance state representation and value estimation. Wang et al. [30] 
improved policy stability in the LocoMamba framework by performing hierarchical mod-
eling of environmental states. Mustafa et al. [31] introduced the Mamba module into the 
value network to encode historical state sequences, enabling more efficient navigation. 
Ryu et al. [32] proposed the Mamba-DQN framework, which enhances the accuracy of Q-
value estimation by selectively summarizing state sequences through the Mamba module. 
These findings indicate that Mamba  can improve policy stability and convergence effi-
ciency owing to its efficient long-sequence modeling ability. 
Motivated by the above analysis, this paper focuses on the multi -train virtual cou-
pling control problem and introduces a Mamba -based state prediction module into the 
centralized Critic network of the MASAC (Multi-Agent Soft Actor-Critic) framework. The 
historical state sequence is modeled to generate predictive state representations, thereby 
enhancing modeling capability. Furthermore, a multi-agent aggregated Prioritized Expe-
rience Replay (PER) mechanism and an automatic entropy adjustment strategy are incor-
porated during training to improve sample efficiency, exploration capability, and overall 
training stability. The main contributions of the paper are summarized as follows: 
• A predictive Mamba-based multi-agent soft actor–critic framework (PM-MASAC) is 
proposed. By predicting the next -step state, the control performance of virtual cou-
pled trains under dynamic operating conditions is significantly improved.  
• A hierarchical local–global cooperative reward mechanism is constructed. While en-
suring tracking performance, the mechanism guides agents to focus on the overall

2. Multi-Agent Virtual Train Coupling Decision Modeling 
2.1. Problem Formulation 
The objective of virtual coupling control is for each follower to adjust its position and 
speed to maintain the desired safe distance from the preceding train while synchronizing 
with the leader’s velocity, ultimately achieving a stable, equidistant, and iso -velocity vir-
tual coupling structure. The leader follows a predefined speed profile, serving as the ref-
erence for the formation. The followers acquire necessary state information through vehi-
cle-to-vehicle communication and autonomously decide the acceleration to maintain a 
stable formation while satisfying safety constraints. The virtual coupling environment im-
poses physical constraints, including maximum speed, acceleration, and safety distance  
limits to ensure engineering feasibility.  All trains operate along a single track in one di-
rection, considering only longitudinal dynamics and neglecting lateral motion or turnout 
switching. 
A simulation environment for multi -agent reinforcement learning (MARL) is con-
structed to evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed algorithm in virtual train coupling 
control tasks. The environment consists of three trains: a leader and two followers (Fol-
lower 1 and Follower 2), forming a typical longitudinal train formation scenario. Figure 1 
illustrates the train control model based on the VBTC system. 
However, the proposed framework is not limited to this configuration. The system is 
formulated in a decentralized multi-agent manner, where each following train makes de-
cisions based only on local observations . Such a framework ensures that the proposed 
method can be extended to larger-scale coupling by increasing the number of agents, with-
out requiring modifications to the policy structure or learning mechanism.

In the proposed PM -MASAC framework, each train is treated as an independent 
agent. The training follows the centralized training and decentralized execution (CTDE) 
paradigm. Specifically, each agent maintains an independent actor network, meaning that 
policy parameters are not shared across agents. This design allows each agent to learn i ts 
own control policy based on local observations.  Additionally, t he entropy -regularized 
policy optimization mechanism introduced in MASAC ensures sufficient exploration, 
thereby improving policy robustness. 
For the critic, a centralized structure is adopted, where each agent is equipped with 
its own critic network that takes the global state and joint actions of all agents as input. 
Therefore, while the critic utilizes centralized information during training,  the critic net-
works are not shared among agents. 
To capture the temporal evolution characteristics of train operation state, a Mamba-
based state prediction module is incorporated into the framework. This module predicts 
future states

more frequently, thereby accelerating policy convergence and improving training stability.

To comprehensively evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed method, this study

sensitivity analysis. Subsequently, comparative experiments and ablation studies are con-

combined synergistic effects. Finally, the overall performance and generalization capabil-
ity of the proposed algorithm are systematically evaluated using both the training dataset

4.5. Impact of Observation Delay on System Performance 
In real-world railway operations, information exchange among trains is constrained 
by communication conditions, where state information cannot always be obtained instan-
taneously. Such non -ideal sensing and information conditions introduce delayed obser-
vation feedback, which may affect the decision-making accuracy and control performance 
of multi-train cooperative strategies.

5. Conclusions 
To address the challenges of complex multi -train interactions and significant long -
term temporal dependencies in train virtual coupling control, this paper proposes a Pre-
dictive Mamba-based Multi-Agent Soft Actor-Critic (PM-MASAC) control framework un-
der the Centralized Training and Decentralized Execution (CTDE) architecture. By intro-
ducing the Mamba prediction model to construct enhanced state representations for the 
centralized Critic network, significantly improv ing the modeling capacity of the system. 
Integrated with a prioritized experience replay mechanism and an automatic entropy ad-
justment strategy, the proposed method enhances sample efficiency and exploration sta-
bility within continuous action spaces. Furthermore, a hierarchical reward function fo r 
multi-agent virtual coupling tasks is designed to guide agents in learning coordinated car-
following control strategies. 
Simulation experiments were conducted across various non -stationary simulation 
scenarios consistent with high-speed railway operation characteristics. Compared to typ-
ical multi -agent reinforcement learning algorithms such as MASAC, MADDPG, and 
MATD3, the proposed PM-MASAC framework achieves more stable speed and distance 
tracking control under complex operating conditions. Moreover, it achieves superior com-
prehensive performance in key indicators such as tracking accuracy, ride comfort, and 
steady-state compliance of the virtual coupling distance. These results verify the effective-
ness of the introduced Mamba-based state prediction mechanism in enhancing the stabil-
ity and robustness of multi-agent cooperative control. 
In summary, this paper presents a cooperative control framework that combines pre-
dictive models with multi-agent reinforcement learning for the train virtual coupling con-
trol problem from the perspectives of system state evolution modeling, improving control 
stability in complex collaborative scenarios. 
In terms of simulation environment modeling for data -driven training, future re-
search could be extended in two directions. First, adopting a multi -mass model that cap-
tures inertial effects between carriages to enable finer motion control. Second, moving be-
yond simulation-based modeling to directly leverage real train operational data for train-
ing agents and learning control policies. In addition, dynamically increasing inter -train 
spacing and incorporating routing-based decoupling strategies near stations represent im-
portant and practical extensions of the current framework, which can improve operational 
flexibility and safety in real-world deployment scenarios. On the algorithmic side, future 
work will further investigate longer prediction horizons and mult i-scale state modeling 
methods, while validating the framework’s generalization capability and engineering ap-
plicability under large-scale coupling and complex uncertain disturbances.
