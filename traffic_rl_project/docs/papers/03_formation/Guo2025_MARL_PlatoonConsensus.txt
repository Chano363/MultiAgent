Abstract
Coordinating a platoon of connected and automated vehicles significantly
improvestrafficefficiencyandsafety.Currentplatooncontrolmethodsprioritize
consistency and convergence performance but overlook the inherent interde-
pendencebetweentheplatoonandthethenon-connectedleadingvehicle.This
oversightconstrainstheplatoon’sadaptabilityincar-followingscenarios,result-
ing in suboptimal optimization performance. To address this issue, this paper
proposed a platoon control framework based on multi-agent reinforcement
learning, aiming to integrate cooperative optimization with platoon tracking
behavior and internal coordination strategies. This strategy employs a bidirec-
tionalcooperativeoptimizationmechanismtoeffectivelydecoupletheplatoon’s
tracking behavior from its internal coordination control, and then recouple it
in a multi-objective optimized manner. Additionally, it leverages long short-
termmemorynetworkstoaccuratelycaptureandmanagetheplatoon’sdynamic
nature over time, aiming to achieve enhanced optimization outcomes. The
simulation results demonstrate that the proposed method effectively improves
the platoon’s cooperative effect and car-following adaptability. Compared to
the consensus control strategy, it reduces the average spacing error by 8.3%.
Furthermore,theaveragelengthoftheplatoondecreasesby19.1%.

Model-based approaches typically employ nonlinear con-
trolstrategiestocharacterizevehicledynamicsandcapture

data-driven robust predictive control method, which has
been demonstrated to effectively solve traffic oscillations
and improve traffic efficiency. Their results demonstrate
the effectiveness of the proposed approach in achieving
platoon convergence and maintaining consistent control.
Despite the advantages of model-based control methods
in improving platoon stability, developing accurate and
reliablemodelsforthelongitudinalmotionofvehiclepla-
toonsremainsasignificantchallengeowingtothecomplex

Learning-based methodologies, characterized by their

formance in ITS control applications (Karim & Adeli,
2003;R a f i e ie ta l . ,2024a, 2024b). Current research iden-
tifies three dominant learning-based control paradigms:
Iterativelearning-basedcontrol(ILC;Dekkeretal., 2019),
neuralnetwork(NN)-basedadaptivecontrol(X.Ma, 2006),
and RL-based control (RL). NN and their derived con-
trollersarelimitedtomimickingtheparameteradjustment
strategies shown by experts, and their ability to general-
ize must be validated. While ILC requires minimal prior
knowledge, it suffers from computational latency that
impedes real-time implementation, posing challenges for
real-time control. RL achieves effective control outcomes
by learningand enhancingperformancethroughinterac-

recent years. A comparative analysis (Yuan Lin et al.,
2021)wasconductedtoevaluatetheRLandmodel-based

longitudinal car-following control algorithm based on a
deepdeterministicpolicygradient(DDPG)RLalgorithm.

5 CONCLUSIONANDFUTUREWORK
ThispaperinvestigatedtheintegrationofMARLandCCS
to address platoon control challenges while preserving
the coupling relationship between internal coordination
within the platoon and the exogenous vehicle. Multi-
agent Markov game theory created a joint target value
function estimated by LSTM to capture time-relatedness
in platoon control. The platoon’s performance improved,
convergencestabilityimproved,andtrafficflowefficiency
enhanced through training in a mixed driving scenario.
Theprimaryconclusionsaresummarizedasfollows:
1. TheLSTM-MAPPOalgorithmachievesa19.13%reduc-
tion in average platoon length compared to CCS,
attributedtothebidirectionalcontrolmechanism.
2. The incorporation of the LSTM network has resulted
in improved traffic outflow performance compared to
the original CCS method. This improvement is evi-
dent across various scenarios, with a 20.1% increase
in optimization effect observed in low-speed scenar-
ios.Theproposedcontrolstrategyhasdemonstratedan
8.3% reduction in spacing error control in high-speed
scenarios.
While the LSTM-MAPPO framework enhances pla-
toon coordination, three limitations persist: first, homo-
geneous assumptions misrepresent mixed fleets. Sec-
ond,V2Xuncertaintiesdegradedecisionsynchronization.
Third, labeled data dependency limits scenario general-
ization. Future work will explore the following: neural
dynamic classification for heterogeneous interaction pat-
tern recognition through dynamic weighting; dynamic
ensemble learning for robust multi-sensor fusion under
communicationdisturbances;finiteelementmachinesfor
sparse state representation; and self-supervised strategies
for latent feature extraction. These aim to build uni-
versal cooperative control while retaining computational
efficiency.
