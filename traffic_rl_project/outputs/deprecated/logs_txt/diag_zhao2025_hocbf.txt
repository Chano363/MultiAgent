Abstract -- High-speed maglev train levitation systems face 
significant stability and safety challenges due to their inherent 
nonlinear open-loop instabilit y and the complex operating 
environment caused by aerody namic lift force and track 
irregularities. Existing model-based control methods rely on 
precise mathematical models and manual parameter tuning, 
struggling to adapt to comp lex dynamic environments. 
Learning-based approaches, mean while, suffer from difficulties 
in convergence under strong disturbances and insufficient 
safety guarantees. To address these limitations, this paper 
proposes a safe reinforcement learning control method based on 
higher-order control barrier functions (HOCBF) and 
disturbance observer (DOB). The proposed method employs a 
hierarchical design: reinforcement learning (RL) adaptively 
learns the optimal policy from data; HOCBF construct a safety 
layer to modify the RL agent's actions, ensuring system safety; 
and the DOB compensates for external disturbances like 
aerodynamic lift, enhancing co nvergence stability under strong 
disturbances. Simulation results verify the efficacy of the 
presented approach under three operating conditions, 
confirming remarkable improvements in convergence 
performance under strong disturbances and enhanced stability. 
 
Index Terms -- Maglev train, Levitation system, Safe 
reinforcement learning, Control barrier function, Disturbance 
observer.  
 
I.   INTRODUCTION 
lectromagnetic Suspension (EMS) type high-speed 
maglev trains achieve contactless support, guidance, 
traction, and braking through electromagnetic forces between 
the vehicle and the track. Maglev trains obtain power through 
non-contact energy transfer , overcoming the two key 
limitations constraining the speed of traditional wheel-rail 
trains: adhesion utilization and sliding current collection [1], 
[2]. Consequently, maglev trains have become a research 
hotspot for next-generation ultra-high-speed trains. 
Theoretically, high-speed maglev trains offer an 
economically viable operating speed of up to 600 km/h, 
effectively bridging the speed gap between high-speed rail 
and air transport [3]. 
The levitation subsystem serves as the core component 
ensuring contactless and safe operation of high-speed maglev 
trains, and its operational performance directly impacts the 
train’s safety and passenger ride comfort [4]. The levitation

Furthermore, electromagnets can only provide unidirectional 
upward force, stable levitation relies on the counteracting 
force of gravity [5]. The operating environment is complex, 
subject to aerodynamic lift forces  and track irregularities. At 
high speeds, the aerodynamic lif t force increases rapidly. Its 
counteraction to gravity causes a rapid reduction in the 
levitation gap, significantly increasing the risk of collision 
accidents between the train and track. [6]. 
Numerous researchers have proposed solutions from the 
perspective of levitation control algorithms. For instance, 
considering the imp act of unsteady periodic aerodynamic 
forces on the levitation system's  dynamic response, Liu et al. 
[7] identified bifurcation parameter intervals for control 
gains in full-state feedback control. Wang et al. [8] analyzed 
the influence of control gain parameters on system stability 
under random excitation induced by track irregularities, 
determining recommended value ranges for proportional 
derivative (PD) control parameters. 
Beyond classical control meth ods like full-state feedback 
and PD control, modern control techniques have been widely 
applied to enhance levitation system robustness. For example, 
considering voltage saturation, sensor noise, and various 
parameter uncertainties in the levitation system, Li et al. [9] 
developed a self-tuning double-layer SMC system capable of 
achieving high-performance control with relatively low 
control voltages under significant model uncertainty and 
disturbances, validated experimentally. Considering 
constraints on gap, velocity, an d acceleration, Hu et al. [10] 
proposed a robust MPC strate gy, efficiently solving the 
optimization problem using a primal-dual interior-point 
algorithm to handle constraints in real-time, with 
effectiveness demonstrated vi a simulation. Furthermore, 
incorporating vehicle-track coupled vibration and control 
loop faults, Guo et al. [11] established a coupled vehicle-
track model, analyzed the dynamic response performance of 
the levitation system under different fault conditions, and 
validated the model's accuracy by comparing it with 
measured train-viaduct responses. 
State observers, which reconstruct unmeasurable system 
states from measurable outputs,  provide more complete state 
information for controller design [12]. The application of 
sliding mode observers [13], extended state observers [14], 
and Kalman filter observers [15] has further improved the 
control accuracy and disturbance rejection capability of 
levitation systems under complex operating conditions. For 
instance, considering track irregularity excitation, Jiang et al. 
[16] designed an adaptive disturbance observer to 
compensate for and suppress irregularity disturbances, 
achieving stable levitation of a half-suspension frame

Zhu et al. [17] proposed an adaptive SMC method based on a 
linear extended state observer (LESO). The LESO estimates 
the system's total disturbance and unmodeled dynamics in 
real-time, which are then canceled within the SMC. 
Simulations and experiments verified the controller's strong

Unlike the model-based methods above, learning-based 
methods can adaptively learn optimal control policies 
directly from data, freeing them from reliance on manual 
tuning and expert experience, thus offering better 
adaptability. Representative learning-based methods include 
iterative learning control [18], neural network control [19], 
and reinforcement learning control (RL) [20]. Particularly, 
deep RL, with its capability to learn complex policies in 
high-dimensional environments , has achieved remarkable 
success and become a powerful  approach for addressing 
challenges in levitation systems. For example, Zhu et al. [21] 
proposed a multi-agent RL cooperative levitation controller, 
with simulations indicating its effectiveness in handling 
coupling effects between levitation units. While these 
methods yield promising results, the following issues remain 
unresolved: 
1) In model-based methods, controller design heavily 
depends on accurate mathematical models of the levitation 
system, and performance is significantly influenced by 
manual tuning and expert experience. Adapting 
hyperparameters to accommodat e the changing dynamics of 
the levitation system in complex environments is challenging. 
2) Learning-based methods typically employ an end-to-
end approach to directly solve for the optimal policy. 
However, under strong disturbances like aerodynamic loads, 
RL algorithms struggle to converge and find the optimal 
solution. 
3) Current RL algorithms designed for levitation systems 
lack safety guarantees, unab le to prevent the agent from 
taking unsafe actions during random exploration. 
Notably, the concept of safe RL has been explored in 
other domains. For instance, Em am et al. [22] formulated a 
constrained optimization problem based on control barrier 
functions (CBF) to ensure safety during training. Han et al. 
[23] constructed cost constraints based on Lyapunov theory, 
theoretically guaranteeing the stability of the control system 
upon RL convergence. Synthesizing the above analysis, this 
paper focuses on the disturbance caused by aerodynamic lift 
on high-speed maglev train levitation systems and proposes a 
safe RL control method based on higher-order CBF (HOCBF) 
and disturbance observer (DOB). Guided by a hierarchical 
design principle, actions genera ted by the RL agent are first 
corrected by a safety layer constructed using HOCBF. 
Additionally, a DOB is designed to compensate for 
aerodynamic loads, ensuring stab le algorithm convergence. 
The main contributions of this paper are: 
1) The proposed method ad aptively learns the optimal 
policy from data, avoiding the high model dependency of 
existing approaches. 
2) The safety layer, designed using HOCBF, theoretically 
guarantees the safety of the levitation system. Furthermore, 
embedding the safety layer into the RL algorithm via 
differentiable optimization accelerates convergence. 
3) Targeted handling of disturbances like aerodynamic 
loads through the DOB mitigates convergence difficulties of 
the RL algorithm caused by strong disturbances, improving 
algorithm stability and control performance. 
The rest of this paper is organized as follows: Section II 
establishes the single-point levitation system model and 
formulates it as a Markov Deci sion Process (MDP). Section 
III introduces the adopted RL algorithm, provides a detailed 
derivation of the proposed HOCBF-based safe RL control 
algorithm for the levitation system, and presents the observer 
design alongside stability proofs. Section IV validates the 
effectiveness of the proposed method under three different 
operating conditions, including aerodynamic load 
disturbances, via simulation. Section V concludes the paper.

V.   CONCLUSION 
Addressing the stability and safety challenges faced by 
high-speed maglev train levitation systems under complex 
disturbances such as aerod ynamic lift forces and track 
irregularities, this paper propo sed a safe RL control method 
integrating HOCBF and DOB. The method employs a 
hierarchical design: the RL agent adaptively learns the 
optimal control policy from data, reducing reliance on 
precise mathematical models; the safety layer constructed 
using HOCBF rigorously guarantees system safety by 
modifying the agent's actions in real-time via differentiable 
optimization; and the DOB sp ecifically compensates for 
external disturbances like aer odynamic loads, significantly 
enhancing the convergence stability and control accuracy of 
the algorithm under strong disturbances. Simulation results 
demonstrate that under three typical operating conditions: 
static levitation, aerodynamic load disturbance alone, and 
combined aerodynamic load w ith track irregularity, the 
proposed method significantly  outperforms traditional PID 
and PID-DOB in key performance metrics such as RMSE 
and maximum error of the levitation air gap. The advantage 
is particularly prominent in rejecting aerodynamic load 
disturbances. This method eff ectively resolves the issues 
inherent in existing model-ba sed approaches and end-to-end 
RL methods, providing a reliable control strategy for the safe 
and stable operation of high-speed maglev trains. Future 
work could involve Hardware-in-the-Loop testing to further 
refine the control strategy.
