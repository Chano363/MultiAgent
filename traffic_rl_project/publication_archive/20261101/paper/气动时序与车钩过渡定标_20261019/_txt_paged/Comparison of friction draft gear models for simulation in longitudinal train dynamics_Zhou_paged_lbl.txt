  Comparison of friction draft gear models for simulation in longitudinal train
                                   dynamics
                               Yichang Zhou, Zhiyong Shi, Hecht M.

Abstract: The friction draft gear with hysteresis characteristics is one of the typical nonlinear
elements in longitudinal train dynamics. Due to nonlinear hysteresis of friction damping, the
discontinuity between loading and unloading envelopes becomes a mathematical integration issue
when adopting look-up table methods to establish draft gear models. It might cause integral diverges
and wrong results if being ignored. To solve this discontinuous problem and pursue a proper
transitional approach, three different transitional strategies (velocity-dependent, intermediate slope,
and smoothing approximation) are applied to building the draft gear model, and the one-dimensional
(1D) longitudinal train model adopting the modified Newmark-ß approach, also called ‘Zhai’
method, is established. In order to ensure accurate results, the international benchmarking cases of
longitudinal train dynamics (LTD) are studied, and dynamic responses for heavy haul trains during
traction and dynamic braking are compared adopting three draft gear models with different
transitional strategies in this paper. The results show that three models can successfully work with
Zhai’s integral method in certain step-sizes to solve longitudinal impulse problems. Compared to
the other two models, the model with the velocity-dependent approach needs a minimum step (0.1
or 0.2 ms) to achieve convergent results, and its average time-costing is approximately eight times
as much time as others. As this method is sensitive to the velocity difference of adjacent wagons,
switching responses can change sharply, and it might cause instabilities in one simulation process.
It is recommended that adopting the intermediate slope method or smoothing approximation
approach to avoid discontinuity problems of modelling draft gears in LTD.

Keywords: draft gear models; transitional strategies; ‘Zhai’ method; benchmark cases; LTD


1. Introduction
With the increase of axle load and train length, the study of longitudinal train dynamics (LTD) has
been an essential element to the railway industry, especially for freight trains, and has been
addressed by worldwide researchers [1]~[4]. The development of LTD includes advanced vehicle
connection models of in-train forces, extended vehicle modelling into multiple degree-of-freedoms
(DOFs), complex nonlinear characteristics of in-vehicle components and other aspects. The wagon
connection system, including draft gears and buffers, directly affects the in-train force, and is one
of the most critical and notable parts for LTD. In fact, the draft gear and buffer system exhibits
strong nonlinear characteristics, including the hysteresis [5], the ‘locked unloading’ behavior [6],
and the varying-stiffness phenomena etc. Therefore, the modelling of this system becomes highly
challenging.
     Early models usually simplify the single wagons as mass points with one degree of freedom,
thereby ignoring the connecting systems between car-bodies and bogies [7][8]. Some other studies
packaged the draft gear into an equivalent simple mass-spring or wedge-spring, failing to represent
the realistic characteristics of corresponding components as well. A refined model was proposed by
Cole[6] based on dropping hammer test data, which presented the varying stiffness as deflection.
The proposed model incorporated the wedge angles and static-dynamic friction and was fitted for

===== [PDF PAGE 3] =====
reproducing the ‘locked unloading’ behavior. An alternative is the look-up table method [9]~[11],
which could reproduce the hysteresis behaviour and improve the computation efficiency, therefore
is widely applied. However, the nonlinear hysteresis entails the discontinuities between loading and
unloading envelopes when applying the look-up table approaches. To fix the discontinuities,
smoothing approximations or transitional characteristics are generally introduced as a mathematical
solution [13]. A recent review paper by Wu on the dynamics modelling of the friction draft gear [5],
summarized the dynamics modelling from different institutions comprehensively, and addressed the
importance of including the velocity-dependent friction, slack, limiting stiffness and transitional
characteristics into draft gear models. Afterwards, productive investigations on advanced draft gear
models have been published by Wu et al. [12].
     Among all existing draft gear models, it is necessary to understand the applicability thoroughly
before adapting them into LTD simulations. The authors compared three representative draft gear
models with different transitional strategies to investigate different methods for modelling the draft
gears. The simulated scenarios are referred from the international benchmarking of LTD simulators
in [15][16]. This paper is organized starting from a detailed introduction on three different
transitional strategies for draft gear modelling, following by a presentation of LTD model developed
in MATLAB adopting the ‘Zhai’ method [22] for numerical integration. Section 4 includes the
simulated results from four benchmark cases using the developed LTD model and comparative
discussion on different methods. The conclusion and outlook are shown in Section 5.




2. Draft gear models
Center-buffer couplers and buffer-hook couplers are two types of coupling connection systems for
freight wagons in the world’s railway system. And Center-buffer couplers are popularly used in
America, China [23][24] and Australia [5] while buffer-hook couplers are widely applied in the
European railway system [25]. Early draft gear models for both types of couplers are simplified as
a combination of linear stiffness and damping, ignoring the nonlinear hysteresis characteristics of
the friction damping. Recent developments have incorporated different considerations into
advanced draft gear models, widely using the look-up table approach.
      A look-up table approach generally includes fixed tables to represent the force-deflection
features of draft gears from tests, and adopts different simplifications to define the transitional
strategies. As the force-deflection characteristic is dependent on the type of draft gear, the
transitional characteristics affect the performance of a draft gear model significantly. To investigate
the performance of different modelling methods, three draft gear models with different transitional
strategies are compared in this part.

3. Longitudinal train dynamics model
To compare above mentioned friction draft gear models, we have applied different draft gear models
into longitudinal train dynamics simulator to investigate the dynamical influence. Figure 3 illustrates
one typical LTD model.

     As shown in Figure 3, the running direction degree of freedom of each vehicle, represented by
multibody dynamical components, is included. A detailed explanation regarding the model is
introduced in Section 3.2.

3.1 Architecture of the LTD simulator
A general architecture of the LTD simulator is shown in Figure 4. Five parts are included, pre-
processing, initialization, algorithm, net force, and post-processing. The simulator is developed in
MATLAB. The first part aims to discrete input parameters, including track discretization, buffer F-
D discretization, theoretical traction and dynamic braking force discretization for locomotives, and
driving control sequences discretization. For example, the track curves and gradients information
are interpolated by a fixed discretization step in the pre-processing stage. Without judgement or loop
statements, the fixed discretization step is an effective method to save computing resources in
MATLAB, and the steps of track sections, buffer-coupling system deflections, locomotive speed in
the traction and DB notches, and driving control sequences are 1 m, 0.01 mm, 1 mm/s, and 1 ms,
respectively [20].
      The second part initializes other simulation conditions, such as the train configuration, mass
matrix, locomotive and coupler positions. It should be noted that the locomotive position is relevant
to traction force and dynamic braking force while the coupler position is connected with different
slacks between the coupler system and draw-bar system. The third part is the core of this program
including ‘Zhai’ integration method [22], and the displacements, velocities and accelerations of
vehicles are calculated in this component. The fourth part pursues to obtain the net force, such as
track resistance and running resistance for each time step, and pass the total force to the third part.
The last part of this simulator is the post-processing including outputs of speed, maximum in-train
force and buffer F-D envelopes.

3.3 Numerical integration method
Newmark- ß is an approach of numerical integration to solve certain differential equations, and was
developed by Nathan M. Newmark in 1959 for solving response of large-scale structural systems
[14]. It is a typical implicit method with second-order accuracy, and are widely applied to dynamic
systems for its high precision. However, it costs extra computing resource due to the implicit
integration method. To solve this problem, Zhai [22] proposed a simple explicit method with second-
order accuracy to solve large-scale nonlinear dynamic issues based on the Newmark- ß approach,
and validated this new method in railway engineering. The modified method, also called ‘Zhai’
method, is expressed by Equation (9) and (10)

4.4 Discussion
The results from the table 1 summarize the simulation results for four cases adopting three different
draft gear modelling methods. The calculated in-train force and the deflection of selected coupler
position using three different draft gear models can reach well agreements with referenced results.
     The results of Case 3 are similar to Case 1, whereas those of Case 4 are close to Case 2 due to
similar train configurations. Compared with Case 1, the maximum compressive force occurred at
nearly the three-fifth of the train length in Case 3 during train operations. The largest tensile and
compressive forces in Case 2 and 4 all arose near to the locomotives in the middle of the train.
Among all the cases, the average of maximum tensile and compressive force adopting model 1 is
the lowest whereas the maximum buffer deflections of chosen positions are larger than others.

5. Conclusions

===== [PDF PAGE 13] =====
In this paper, three representative draft gear models with different transitional strategies to
investigate different methods for modelling the draft gears were compared. The LTD simulator
including the draft gear models were presented in detail. Based on the ‘Zhai’ integral method,
developed in MATLAB, the simulator enables the analysis of longitudinal dynamics problems. The
following conclusions can be obtained:
     (1) The three transitional methods (velocity-dependent, intermediate slope, and smoothing
         approximation) can successfully cooperate with ‘Zhai’ integral method, a two-step explicit
         method, to solve the longitudinal impulse problems in LTD, and the results are in good
         agreement with the benchmark results. The dynamic responses (including results and
         calculating time of four cases) of intermediate slope and smoothing approximation have
         tiny divergences.
     (2) The velocity-dependent method requires a minimum integration step (0.1 ms or 0.2 ms) to
         get stable responses while the step sizes of other two approaches are only 1 ms. Adopting
         the intermediate slope or smoothing approximation method can save more calculating
         resources and realize online simulations.
     (3) The velocity-dependent method is sensitive with the velocity difference of adjacent wagons
         while other two approaches are controlled by the relative displacement of adjacent wagons.
         If the velocity sharply changes, it might cause instabilities in the simulation process. Hence,
         the intermediate slope method and smoothing approximation approach are recommended
         to deal with the discontinuity problems of modeling frictional draft gears in LTD.
     (4) The maximum buffer deflections of the velocity-dependent method are obviously higher
         than others, and the highest in-train forces are obtained in the transitional zone due to its
         smoothing strategy. This explains the reason why its highest in-train forces are almost equal
         to others although the buffer deflections are obviously larger.
      Apart from approaches introduced in this paper, there still remain other methods to establish
the draft gear model and further comparisons are supposed to execute in the future.
