# Results Section — Move-Step Framework

Based on the textbook's Table 10.1 Move Analysis and Swales & Feak conventions.

---

## Move 1: Preparatory Information

**Purpose:** Orient the reader before presenting data. Provide the source, scope, and any necessary context for the results that follow.

### Steps:

**1a — Source of data**
State where the results come from — which dataset, table, instrument, or participant group.
- Tense: **past simple** (data was obtained in the past)
- *"Information on unsupervised driving was obtained for 2,958 members (74%) of the cohort."*
- *"The data presented here were collected from the experimental group only."*

**1b — Defining / explaining the data structure**
Clarify how data is organized or how a variable was split if this is not obvious.
- *"This group was divided into two parts: those with 1–12 unsupervised trips and those with 13+ trips."*
- *"For the purposes of this analysis, participants were classified into three age groups."*

**1c — Stating the aim of the analysis**
Sometimes authors restate what the results will address.
- *"The following results address the first research question regarding…"*
- *"Table 1 presents the associations between demographic variables and…"*

---

## Move 2: Reporting Results

**Purpose:** Present the actual findings. This is the core Move of the Results section.

Move 2 typically cycles: for each new finding or data block, the sequence repeats.

### Step sequence within Move 2:

**2a — Locating results (obligatory)**
Point the reader to the table, figure, or data source.
- Tense: **present simple** (the table currently exists and shows)
- *"Table 1 shows / presents / displays…"*
- *"As shown in Figure 2…"*
- *"The results are presented in Table 3."*

**2b — Explaining the result (if needed)**
Clarify what is being shown before stating the finding.
- *"In these models, if we found a factor was associated in one group but not the other, we nevertheless included the factor in the second model so that readers can compare the direction of effect."*

**2c — Reporting the result (obligatory)**
State the actual finding — with specifics (numbers, percentages, test statistics, p-values).
- Tense: **past simple** (the result was obtained in the past)
- Voice: **active preferred** ("We found…", "Results showed…")
- *"Thirty-one percent of the study sample was non-compliant at least once."*
- *"Female children showed higher average rates of physically interactive play (M = 22.4) than males (M = 10.7)."*
- *"The risk associated with frequent unsupervised driving was significantly higher (RR = 5.02, CI: 2.96–8.51)."*

**2d — Commenting on the result (optional)**
Highlight the most notable aspect of a finding. This is descriptive, not interpretive.
- *"Overall, the most notable finding was the increasing risk associated with distance driven."*
- *"These results show that female subjects showed a higher tendency to display physically interactive group play."*
- *"Notably, this pattern was consistent across all three age groups."*

**Important boundary:** Commentary in the Results section is **descriptive** (what the pattern is) — it does NOT interpret (why the pattern exists) or compare with prior literature. Those functions belong in the Discussion.

---

## Recurring Move 2 Pattern

For papers with multiple findings or analyses, Move 2 repeats:

```
[Finding block 1]
→ Locate (Table 1 shows…)
→ Report (31% of participants…)
→ Comment (The most notable aspect was…)

[Finding block 2]
→ Locate (Table 2 presents…)
→ Explain (These models were constructed to…)
→ Report (Factors associated with both levels were…)
→ Comment (Overall, risk was substantially greater for…)
```

---

## Results vs. Discussion — The Boundary

This is the most critical distinction in polishing the Results section.

| Belongs in Results | Belongs in Discussion |
|-------------------|----------------------|
| "Female children showed higher rates of play." | "This may be because females are more socially oriented." |
| "The risk was 5× higher for frequent breachers." | "This is consistent with Smith (2020), who found…" |
| "Overall, most factors were related to non-compliance." | "These findings suggest that policy interventions should…" |
| "The result was statistically significant (p = .02)." | "The statistical significance confirms our hypothesis that…" |

**Test:** Can a sentence stand alone as a fact about what was found? → Results. Does it explain why, compare with literature, or draw implications? → Discussion.

---

## Commentary Types (All Descriptive)

| Commentary function | Example |
|--------------------|---------|
| Highlighting a pattern | *"In general, risk was substantially greater for frequent breachers."* |
| Noting an exception | *"The only exception was age, which was not significantly associated in the 1–12 group."* |
| Summarizing across findings | *"Overall, these results show that female subjects showed a higher tendency for physical play."* |
| Flagging a notable result | *"The most notable finding was the strong association with distance driven."* |

---

## Table and Figure Reference Conventions

**Announcing a table/figure (before it appears):**
- *"The results are presented in Table 1."*
- *"Figure 1 displays the frequency distribution of…"*

**Referring to a table/figure while reporting:**
- *"As shown in Table 2, the majority of factors…"*
- *"Table 3 shows the bivariate associations between…"*

**After referring, report the key finding — don't just say "see Table 1."**

---

## Common Errors

- ❌ Starting the section with a result and no Move 1 orientation
- ❌ Vague results: "Results were significant" → specify: "Results were significant (p = .03, d = 0.62)"
- ❌ Interpretation in Results: "This suggests that X is caused by Y" → move to Discussion
- ❌ Comparing with literature in Results: "Unlike Smith (2020)…" → move to Discussion
- ❌ Just saying "see Table 1" without reporting what the table shows
- ❌ Not reporting figures/tables at all (they should always be announced and summarized)
- ❌ Mixing past and present tense: use past for findings, present for table references
