# Language Features — Discussion Section

---

## Tense and Voice by Move

| Move / Step | Tense | Voice | Notes |
|-------------|-------|-------|-------|
| Move 1 — Restate purpose | Past simple | Active | *"This study aimed to…"* |
| Move 2 — Report results | Past simple | Active preferred | *"Results showed that…"* |
| Move 3 — Summarize results | Present simple | Active | *"Overall, the findings suggest…"* |
| Move 4a — Interpret results | Present simple + hedges | Active | *"This may indicate that…"* |
| Move 4b — Compare with literature (their findings) | Past simple | Active or passive | *"Smith (2020) found…"* |
| Move 4b — Compare (the comparison itself) | Present simple | Active | *"This is consistent with…"* |
| Move 4c — Account for discrepancy | Present simple + hedges | Active | *"This may be due to…"* |
| Move 5 — Overall conclusion | Present simple | Active | *"This study demonstrates…"* |
| Move 6a — Limitations | Past simple / past perfect | Passive preferred | *"Generalizability was limited by…"* |
| Move 6b — Strengths | Present simple / present perfect | Active | *"This study contributes…"* |
| Move 7 — Suggestions / future work | Modal verbs | Active | *"Future studies should…"* |
| Move 7 — Implications | Present simple | Active | *"These findings have implications for…"* |

---

## Modal Verbs — When and Which

Modals are critical in the Discussion section for expressing degrees of certainty and making recommendations.

| Modal | Meaning | Typical use |
|-------|---------|------------|
| **should** | Strong recommendation | *"Future studies should include…"* |
| **could** | Possibility / suggestion | *"This difference could be explained by…"* |
| **may** | Epistemic uncertainty | *"These results may reflect…"* |
| **might** | Weaker uncertainty | *"Differences might be attributed to…"* |
| **would** | Hypothetical / conditional | *"A larger sample would strengthen…"* |
| **can** | Capability | *"These findings can inform…"* |

**Modal density is highest in Moves 4c, 6a, and 7** — where claims are interpretive or forward-looking.

## Hedging

Hedging signals epistemic caution — that a claim is an interpretation, not an established fact.

### Required hedging contexts
- Interpreting results (Move 4a): *"These results suggest / indicate / appear to show…"*
- Accounting for discrepancies (Move 4c): *"This may be due to… / could reflect… / might be attributed to…"*
- Implications (Move 7c): *"These findings may have implications for…"*
- Limitations on generalizability: *"These results may not generalize to…"*

### Prohibited hedging contexts
- Reporting what was found (Move 2): do not hedge established results
  - ❌ *"Results may show that females engaged in more play."*
  - ✅ *"Results showed that females engaged in significantly more play."*

### Hedging devices
| Type | Examples |
|------|---------|
| Hedging verbs | *suggest, indicate, appear, seem, tend to, point to* |
| Modal verbs | *may, might, could, would* |
| Adverbs | *possibly, probably, likely, apparently, seemingly* |
| Limiting phrases | *in this sample, under these conditions, to some extent* |
| Attribution | *it is possible that, it seems that, there is reason to believe* |

## Lexical Signals by Move

### Move 1 — Background
- *The aim / purpose of this study was to…*
- *This study investigated / examined / sought to…*

### Move 2 — Reporting results
- *The findings showed / revealed / indicated that…*
- *[N]% of participants / subjects…*
- *The most notable finding was…*

### Move 3 — Summarizing
- *Overall, these results suggest / indicate…*
- *Taken together, the findings point to…*
- *In summary, the data show that…*
- *The pattern of results is consistent with…*

### Move 4a — Interpreting
- *These results suggest / indicate that…*
- *This finding may be explained by…*
- *One possible interpretation is that…*
- *This pattern is consistent with the view that…*
- *This finding is important because…*

### Move 4b — Comparing with literature
Agreement: *consistent with / in line with / support / corroborate / confirm*
Disagreement: *contrary to / in contrast to / unlike / differ from / contradict*
- *These findings are consistent with Smith (2020), who found that…*
- *This result is contrary to Jones (2018), who reported…*
- *Unlike previous studies, the present research found…*

### Move 4c — Accounting for discrepancies
- *This discrepancy may be due to…*
- *A possible explanation is that…*
- *The difference might be attributed to…*
- *This could reflect differences in sample composition / methodology / context…*

### Move 5 — Overall conclusion
- *In conclusion / To summarize…*
- *Overall, this study demonstrates / shows / provides evidence for…*
- *The present study has shown that…*

### Move 6a — Limitations
- *One limitation of this study is…*
- *The generalizability of these findings is limited by…*
- *The study was unable to control for…*
- *The small sample size (n = X) limits…*

### Move 6b — Strengths
- *A strength of this study is…*
- *This is the first study to…*
- *Unlike previous studies, this research…*
- *The study contributes to the literature by…*

### Move 7 — Future research
- *Future studies should…*
- *Further research is needed to…*
- *A longitudinal study would allow…*
- *Future research could address this by…*

### Move 7 — Implications
- *These findings have implications for…*
- *This work has practical implications for…*
- *The results suggest that [practitioner group] should…*
- *These findings pave the way for…*

## Common Errors

- ❌ No hedging in interpretation: "This proves that X causes Y" → "This suggests X may be related to Y"
- ❌ Hedging results: "Results may show that females played more" → remove hedge from reported fact
- ❌ Past tense for interpretation: "These results suggested that…" → "These results suggest that…" (present)
- ❌ Vague literature comparison: "This is consistent with previous research" → name the study
- ❌ "More research is needed" only → specify: what research, on what, why, using what design
- ❌ Overclaiming significance: "This research definitively establishes…" → hedge appropriately
