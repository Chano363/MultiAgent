# Language Features of the Closing Section

---

## Tense and Voice — Full Rules by Move

| Move / Step | Tense | Voice | Rationale |
|-------------|-------|-------|-----------|
| Move 1 — Restate purpose | Past simple | Active preferred | The study happened in the past |
| Move 1 — Review hypothesis | Past simple + modal | Active | "We proposed that X would…" |
| Move 2 — Report results | Past simple | Active preferred | Findings were obtained in the past |
| Move 3 — Interpret results | Present simple | Active preferred | Interpretation is a current claim |
| Move 3 — Compare with literature | Past (their findings) + present (the comparison) | Mixed | Prior studies: past; current relationship: present |
| Move 3 — Account for discrepancies | Present + hedges | Active | Speculative, so hedge appropriately |
| Move 4 — Limitations | Past simple / past perfect | Passive preferred | Design constraints are historical facts |
| Move 4 — Strengths | Present simple / present perfect | Active | Contribution persists into the present |
| Move 4 — Methodology evaluation | Past simple | Passive or active | |
| Move 5 — Suggestions | Modal verbs | Active | Recommendations and possibilities |
| Move 5 — Future research | Modal verbs | Active | *should, could, may* |
| Move 5 — Implications | Present simple | Active | Current relevance |

---

## Modal Verbs

Modal verbs are a defining feature of the closing section, especially in Moves 4 and 5. They convey the appropriate degree of certainty.

| Modal | Meaning in closing sections | Typical use |
|-------|---------------------------|-------------|
| **should** | Obligation / strong recommendation | *Future studies should include larger samples* |
| **could** | Possibility / suggestion | *This method could be extended to other populations* |
| **may** | Epistemic uncertainty | *These results may reflect cultural differences* |
| **might** | Weaker uncertainty | *Differences might be attributed to sample composition* |
| **would** | Hypothetical / conditional | *A randomized design would strengthen these findings* |
| **can** | Capability / potential | *These findings can inform clinical practice* |

**Where modals are most frequent:** Move 5 (deductions) and Move 4 Step 4a (limitations).

**Where modals should be avoided:** Move 2 (reporting results). Do not hedge what was actually found.

## Hedging

Hedging is the use of "vague" or "uncertain" language to signal that a claim is not absolute. The closing section requires hedging for interpretation and implication — but NOT for factual reporting.

### When to hedge

**Interpretation (Move 3):**
> *These results suggest that female children may be more socially oriented in early play…*

**Comparison with literature (Move 3):**
> *This discrepancy could be due to differences in sample composition…*

**Limitations (Move 4):**
> *It is possible that the time of day affected children's behavior…*

**Implications (Move 5):**
> *These findings may have implications for early childhood education policy…*

### When NOT to hedge

**Reporting results (Move 2):**
> ~~Results may show that females engaged in more physical play.~~
> ✅ *Results showed that females engaged in more physical play.*

**Stating established limitations:**
> ~~The sample might have been small.~~
> ✅ *The sample was small (n = 20).*

### Hedging devices

| Device | Examples |
|--------|---------|
| Modal verbs | *may, might, could, would* |
| Epistemic adverbs | *perhaps, possibly, probably, likely* |
| Hedging verbs | *suggest, indicate, appear, seem, tend to* |
| Approximators | *approximately, around, about, roughly* |
| Limiting phrases | *in some cases, under certain conditions, to some extent* |
| Attribution | *it is possible that, there is reason to believe that* |
| Scope limiters | *to our knowledge, in this sample, within this context* |

## Common Lexical Signals by Communicative Purpose

These are the most frequent expressions used for each communicative purpose in closing sections. Use them as models, not templates — vary the phrasing.

### Restating purpose
- *The purpose of this study was to…*
- *This study aimed to / sought to / was designed to…*
- *The present study investigated / examined / explored…*

### Summarizing findings
- *The findings of this study showed / revealed / demonstrated…*
- *Overall, the results indicate / suggest…*
- *The data showed that…*
- *We found that…*

### Interpreting results
- *These results suggest / indicate that…*
- *This finding may be explained by…*
- *One possible interpretation is that…*
- *This pattern is consistent with the view that…*

### Comparing with literature
- *These findings are consistent with / in line with…*
- *This result is contrary to / differs from…*
- *Unlike [Author], who found…, the present study found…*
- *While [Author] reported X, our results showed Y…*

### Indicating limitations
- *One limitation of this study is…*
- *The generalizability of these findings is limited by…*
- *This study was unable to control for…*
- *The small sample size (n = X) limits…*
- *Future replications should address…*

### Indicating strengths
- *A key strength of this study is…*
- *This is the first study to…*
- *The study contributes to…*
- *Unlike previous studies, the present research…*

### Making recommendations / future research
- *Future studies should…*
- *Further research is needed to…*
- *It is recommended that future work…*
- *A larger / longitudinal / cross-cultural study would…*
- *Future research could address this by…*

### Drawing implications
- *These findings have implications for…*
- *This work contributes to our understanding of…*
- *The results suggest that practitioners should…*
- *These findings pave the way for…*
- *This approach could be extended to…*

## Common Errors in Language Use

- ❌ Hedging results: "Results may show females engaged in more play" (results either showed it or didn't)
- ❌ No hedges in interpretation: "This proves that girls are more social" (interpretation needs hedging)
- ❌ Vague future research: "More research is needed" (always specify what kind and why)
- ❌ Past tense for implications: "These findings suggested that…" (implications are current → present tense)
- ❌ Using "prove" or "demonstrate conclusively" in Move 5 (overclaiming — use "suggest", "indicate")
- ❌ Passive voice in Move 5: "Future work should be conducted" (active is cleaner: "Future studies should…")
