# Move-Step Rules for the Closing Section

Detailed rules, lexical signals, and examples for each Move and Step.

---

## Move 1: Preparatory Information

**Purpose:** Orient the reader by restating what the study set out to do and what was expected. Bridges from the body of the paper to the evaluation.

### Step 1a — Restating the purpose
- Paraphrase the original research objective — do not copy from the Introduction
- 1–2 sentences maximum
- Tense: **past simple** ("The purpose of this study was to…")

**Lexical signals:**
*The purpose of this study was to… / This study aimed to… / This paper investigated… / The goal of this research was to…*

**Example:**
> The purpose of this study was to examine the differences in frequencies of physically interactive group play between male and female children enrolled at a research-oriented preschool.

### Step 1b — Reviewing the hypothesis
- State the original hypothesis if one was made
- Tense: **past simple** with modal (*"We proposed that… would…"*)
- Only include if the paper explicitly stated a hypothesis

**Lexical signals:**
*We proposed that… / It was hypothesized that… / We predicted that… / The hypothesis was that…*

---

## Move 2: Reporting Results

**Purpose:** Briefly summarize the main findings as a transition into commentary. This is a condensed version of the Results section — do not repeat in full.

- 1–3 sentences covering the most important outcomes
- Include specific data if it aids clarity, but do not list all results
- Tense: **past simple**
- Voice: **active preferred** (*"We found…"*, *"The findings showed…"*)

**Lexical signals:**
*The findings of this study… / Results showed that… / We found that… / The data revealed… / Overall, the study demonstrated…*

**Example:**
> The findings of this study did not support the hypothesis, showing that female children displayed higher average rates of physically interactive play than males.

**Common error:** Writing a full re-summary of the results section. Move 2 should be 1–3 sentences max — its purpose is transition, not repetition.

---

## Move 3: Commenting on Results

**Purpose:** Interpret, explain, and evaluate the findings. This is the intellectual core of the closing section. Contains up to four Steps which may recur across multiple findings.

### Step 3a — Interpreting results
- What do the results mean? Why did this happen?
- Connect findings to theory, context, or mechanisms
- Tense: **present simple** (the interpretation is a current claim)
- Voice: **active preferred**

**Lexical signals:**
*These results suggest that… / This finding indicates… / This may be because… / One possible explanation is… / This pattern reflects…*

### Step 3b — Comparing results with literature
- Are findings consistent with or contrary to previous studies?
- Identify specific prior work and explain agreements or discrepancies
- Tense: **past simple** for what prior studies found; **present** for the comparison itself

**Lexical signals (agreement):**
*consistent with… / in line with… / support the findings of… / corroborate…*

**Lexical signals (disagreement):**
*contrary to… / in contrast to… / unlike… / differ from… / do not support…*

**Example:**
> Our results showed a higher frequency of physically interactive group play among females, contrary to the findings of Scott and Panksepp (2003), which showed higher rates among males.

### Step 3c — Accounting for unexpected results
- If findings contradict the hypothesis or prior literature, explain why
- Offer methodological, contextual, or theoretical explanations
- Use hedges — these are speculations, not established facts

**Lexical signals:**
*This discrepancy may be due to… / A possible explanation is… / This could reflect… / The difference might be attributed to…*

### Step 3d — Evaluating results overall
- Step back and assess the broader pattern or significance of all findings together
- Tense: **present simple**

**Lexical signals:**
*Overall, these findings suggest… / Taken together, the results indicate… / These findings do not necessarily discredit…*

---

## Move 4: Evaluating the Study

**Purpose:** Assess the study itself — not the findings, but the research design, scope, and contribution.

### Step 4a — Indicating limitations
- Be specific: name actual constraints (sample size, design, scope, population, measurement)
- Avoid generic statements ("this study has some limitations")
- Tense: **past simple** or **past perfect**; passive preferred for methodological constraints
- Use hedges: limitations reduce, not eliminate, validity

**Lexical signals:**
*The external validity of this study was limited by… / One limitation of this study is… / The study was unable to… / The sample size was small… / It was not possible to…*

**Example:**
> The external validity of this study was limited by the small sample size (n = 20) and the lack of random assignment of subjects.

### Step 4b — Indicating strengths / significance
- Balance limitations with what the study does contribute
- Tense: **present simple** or **present perfect**
- Voice: **active preferred**

**Lexical signals:**
*The research has a number of strengths… / This is the first study to… / A strength of this study is… / This study provides… / The findings contribute to…*

**Example:**
> The research has a number of strengths. It is the first prospective study to examine factors associated with unsupervised driving among learner license holders.

### Step 4c — Evaluating methodology (optional)
- Discuss specific methodological choices and their implications
- Include only if methodology was contested or central to the findings

---

## Move 5: Deductions from the Research

**Purpose:** Look forward — what should be done next, and what does this research mean for the field or practice?

### Step 5a — Making suggestions / recommendations
- Concrete, specific suggestions for practitioners, policymakers, or researchers
- Tense: **modal verbs** (should, could, may)

**Lexical signals:**
*Future studies should… / Researchers should consider… / It is recommended that… / Practitioners may wish to…*

### Step 5b — Recommending further research
- Identify specific gaps that remain and what future work should address
- Must follow directly from the limitations identified in Move 4
- Avoid vague statements like "more research is needed"

**Lexical signals:**
*Future studies should incorporate… / Further research is needed to… / A future study could address… / Future research should examine… / It would be valuable to investigate…*

**Example:**
> Future studies should incorporate time of day and location into the research design to control for these confounding variables.

### Step 5c — Drawing implications
- Broader significance for the field, discipline, or society
- Tense: **present simple**; modal verbs for possibility

**Lexical signals:**
*These findings have implications for… / This approach could be applied to… / The results suggest that… / These findings pave the way for…*

---

## Recurring Move Pattern

In longer closing sections, Moves 3–5 may cycle:

```
[Finding A]
→ Move 3: Interpret finding A, compare with literature
→ Move 4: Note a limitation related to finding A
→ Move 5: Suggest future research for finding A

[Finding B]
→ Move 3: Interpret finding B...
→ Move 4: Evaluate the methodology...
→ Move 5: Draw implications...
```

This cycling is normal and expected — do not force a rigid linear structure if the content calls for cycling.
