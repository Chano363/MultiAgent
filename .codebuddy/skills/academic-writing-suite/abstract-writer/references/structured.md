# Structured Abstract Rules

Used in clinical research, empirical social science, psychology, and most medical/science journals.

---

## Tense and Voice by Move

This is the most common source of errors in structured abstracts. Follow these rules per Move:

| Move | Tense | Voice | Notes |
|------|-------|-------|-------|
| Background | Present simple | Active or passive | The gap/problem still exists now |
| Present work / Objective | Past simple or infinitive | Active preferred | "This study investigated…" or "To determine…" |
| Methodology | Past simple | Passive preferred | "Data were collected…" / "Participants were recruited…" |
| Results | Past simple | Active preferred | "We found…" / "Results showed…" — report what happened |
| Conclusion | Present simple | Active preferred | "These findings suggest…" / "X can be used for…" |

Modal verbs (may, might, could) are appropriate in Background (uncertainty) and Conclusion (hedging implications) — avoid them in Results.

---

## IMRAD Format (medical / clinical / natural science / engineering)

Use bold headings. Each heading on its own line before the content.

### Required sections and content:

**Background** (1–2 sentences)
- Why was this study needed? What is the problem or knowledge gap?
- Tense: present simple ("Falls cause significant injury each year.")

**Objective** (1 sentence)
- What did this study aim to do?
- Tense: past simple or infinitive ("This study aimed to…" / "To determine whether…")

**Methods** (2–4 sentences)
- Study design, setting, participants or materials, intervention or exposure, outcome measures
- Enough detail for a reader to assess validity — not a full methods section
- Tense: past simple, passive preferred

**Results** (2–4 sentences)
- Primary findings, with quantitative data where available
- Include effect sizes, confidence intervals, p-values if meaningful
- Report findings only — do not interpret here
- Tense: past simple, active preferred

**Conclusion** (1–2 sentences)
- What do the results mean? What are the clinical or practical implications?
- Must follow directly from the Results — do not overclaim
- Tense: present simple

**Target length:** 250–350 words

**Example skeleton:**
```
Background: [gap/problem — present tense].
Objective: [aim — past or infinitive].
Methods: [design, sample, measures — past passive].
Results: [key findings with data — past active].
Conclusion: [implications — present].
```

## APA-Style Structured Abstract (psychology / social science / education)

### Required sections:

**Objective** — the research question or hypothesis (1 sentence, past tense)
**Method** — participants, design, materials, procedure (2–3 sentences, past passive)
**Results** — primary findings with statistics (2–3 sentences, past active)
**Conclusions** — interpretation and implications (1–2 sentences, present)
**Public Significance Statement** *(optional, some journals require)* — 1–2 plain-language sentences for non-specialist readers; present tense, no jargon

**Target length:** 150–250 words

## Structured Abstract with Non-Standard Headings

Some medical journals (e.g. JAMA) use extended heading sets. Common variants:

| Variant headings | Used in |
|-----------------|---------|
| Context / Objective / Design / Setting / Participants / Interventions / Main Outcome Measures / Results / Conclusions | JAMA |
| Purpose / Materials and Methods / Results / Conclusion | Radiology journals |
| Background / Aims / Methods / Results / Conclusions | Gastroenterology journals |

If the user specifies a target journal, adapt the section headings accordingly while applying the same tense/voice rules above.

---

## Common Errors
- ❌ Results section without any numbers ("results were significant" — specify the data)
- ❌ Conclusions that go beyond what the Results support
- ❌ Methods written in present tense (should be past)
- ❌ Background written in past tense (should be present)
- ❌ Introducing new information in Conclusion not covered in Results
- ❌ Ending with "further research is needed" as the only conclusion
- ❌ Using modal verbs in Results ("results may show…" — results either show or they don't)
