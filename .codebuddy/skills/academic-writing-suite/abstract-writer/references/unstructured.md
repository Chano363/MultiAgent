# Unstructured Abstract Rules

Used in humanities, theoretical papers, literature reviews, conference abstracts, and theses. Single paragraph, no section headings.

---

## Tense and Voice by Move

| Move | Tense | Voice | Notes |
|------|-------|-------|-------|
| Background | Present simple or present perfect | Active or passive | "Research has shown…" / "X remains poorly understood." |
| Present work | Present simple | Active preferred | "This paper argues…" / "We propose…" / "This study examines…" |
| Methodology | Past simple | Passive or active | "Data were collected…" / "We analyzed…" |
| Results | Past simple | Active preferred | "We found…" / "Analysis revealed…" |
| Conclusion | Present simple | Active preferred | "These findings suggest…" / "This approach enables…" |

---

## Informative Unstructured Abstract

All five Moves present in a single flowing paragraph. The most common type for journal articles outside medicine.

**Move sequence and content:**

1. **Background** (1–2 sentences) — establish context and the problem or gap
2. **Present work** (1 sentence) — announce what this paper does; state the objective or argument
3. **Methodology** (1–2 sentences) — describe the approach, data, or method
4. **Results** (2–3 sentences) — report main findings; include specifics (numbers, key outcomes)
5. **Conclusion** (1 sentence) — state the takeaway, contribution, or implication

**Target length by document type:**

| Type | Target |
|------|--------|
| Journal article | 150–250 words |
| Conference paper | 150–200 words |
| Thesis / dissertation | 300–500 words |
| Book chapter | 100–150 words |

---

## Indicative Unstructured Abstract

Covers Moves 1–3 only. Does **not** report results or draw conclusions — only describes the scope and approach.

**When to use:**
- Literature reviews and survey papers
- Theoretical or conceptual papers with no empirical results
- Books or book-length works
- When the user explicitly requests a scope-only summary

**Move sequence:**
1. **Background** (1–2 sentences) — context and problem
2. **Present work** (1–2 sentences) — what this paper covers, argues, or proposes
3. **Scope / Methodology** (2–3 sentences) — what is included, what approach is taken, what sources or frameworks are used

**Language pattern:**
- "This paper reviews / surveys / examines / discusses / presents…"
- "The paper is organized around / covers / addresses…"
- Do NOT write "Results show…" or "We found…" — these signal informative content

**Target length:** 100–200 words

---

## Humanities-Specific Notes

- Lead with the intellectual problem or argument, not the methodology
- The "present work" Move should state a specific claim, not just a topic:
  - ❌ "This paper examines gender in Victorian literature."
  - ✅ "This paper argues that Victorian domestic fiction constructed gendered reading practices that reinforced class mobility anxieties."
- It is expected to name specific texts, authors, cases, or periods studied
- Avoid hedging the argument ("this paper attempts to…" → "this paper argues…")
- Results Move may be framed as "this paper demonstrates / shows / reveals" rather than quantitative findings

---

## Conference Abstract Notes

- Word limits are strict — typically 150–250 words; respect them exactly
- Front-load the argument or main contribution — reviewers read quickly
- All five Moves should be present but compressed
- End with the contribution to the field or conference theme
- Avoid detailed methodology unless it is the main contribution

---

## Thesis / Dissertation Abstract Notes

- Must cover all five Moves; readers should understand the full project from the abstract alone
- Include: research question, methodology, key findings, and contribution to the field
- May briefly mention chapter structure if the thesis is interdisciplinary or unconventional
- Longer than a journal abstract — 300–500 words is standard
- Embed keywords naturally in the text for indexing

---

## Common Errors
- ❌ Describing what the paper does without stating what it finds or argues (indicative when informative is needed)
- ❌ Vague contribution ("contributes to our understanding of X") without saying what specifically was contributed
- ❌ Too much background, not enough results or conclusion
- ❌ Exceeding word limits for conference abstracts
- ❌ Starting with a definition ("According to Merriam-Webster…")
- ❌ Using past tense for the "present work" Move ("this paper argued…" should be "this paper argues…")
