# Executive Summary Rules

Used for business reports, policy documents, and professional deliverables.

## Purpose

An executive summary is not a summary of the document — it is a standalone decision-support document. A senior reader should be able to act on it without reading the full report.

## Structure

### 1. Context / Problem Statement (1–2 sentences)
- What situation or problem prompted this report?
- Why does it matter now?

### 2. Scope and Approach (2–3 sentences)
- What did this report examine?
- What methodology or data sources were used?
- What is the time period or geographic scope?

### 3. Key Findings (3–5 bullet points or short paragraphs)
- Most important discoveries, in descending order of importance
- Include specific data, numbers, and evidence where available
- Each finding should be a complete, standalone statement

### 4. Conclusions (1–2 sentences)
- What do the findings mean overall?
- What is the bottom-line answer to the report's central question?

### 5. Recommendations (2–4 bullet points)
- Specific, actionable steps the reader or organization should take
- Prioritized: most urgent / highest-impact first
- Each recommendation should be linked to a finding

**Target length:** 300–500 words (roughly 10% of the full report, up to a maximum of 2 pages)

---

## Tone and Style

- Direct and confident — no hedging language unless genuine uncertainty exists
- Use active voice and present tense for conclusions and recommendations
- Avoid jargon unless the audience is a specialist group
- Numbers and specifics preferred over vague qualifiers ("17% increase" not "significant increase")
- Do not reference figures, tables, or appendices — the executive summary must stand alone

---

## Formatting Guidance

- Use short paragraphs or bullet points for findings and recommendations
- Bold key terms or numbers sparingly to guide a scanning reader
- Avoid footnotes

---

## Common Errors
- ❌ Summarizing the report's structure rather than its content ("Chapter 3 discusses...")
- ❌ Burying the recommendations at the end with insufficient emphasis
- ❌ Vague recommendations ("further research should be conducted")
- ❌ Longer than 2 pages — edit ruthlessly
- ❌ Repeating the introduction verbatim from the main report
