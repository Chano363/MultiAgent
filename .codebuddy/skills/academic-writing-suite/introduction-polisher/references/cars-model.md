# CARS Model — Create A Research Space (Swales, 1990)

The standard framework for Introduction sections in empirical research articles across all disciplines.

---

## Move 1: Establishing a Territory

**Purpose:** Convince the reader that the research topic is important, active, and worth investigating.

### Step 1 — Claiming centrality
Signal that the topic is important, relevant, or interesting to the field.
- Tense: **present simple** (the topic is currently important)
- Lexical signals: *recently, in recent years, it is widely recognized that, X plays a crucial role in, X is increasingly important…*

### Step 2 — Making topic generalizations
Provide broad background knowledge about the topic.
- Tense: **present simple** for established facts; **present perfect** for recent developments
- Lexical signals: *X is defined as…, X consists of…, it is known that…, studies have shown that…*

### Step 3 — Reviewing prior research (Literature Review)
Survey relevant previous work — what has been done, by whom, with what results.
- Tense: **past simple** for specific study findings; **present perfect** for trends across studies
- Voice: passive common (*"X has been studied by…"*); active also used (*"Smith (2020) found that…"*)
- Lexical signals for reporting: *found, showed, demonstrated, reported, argued, proposed, investigated*
- Lexical signals for evaluating: *clearly shows, stands in contrast to, suggests that, does not account for*

**Move 1 pattern — funnel structure:**
```
Broad claim about importance of topic
↓
Background generalizations
↓
Survey of prior work (what has been done)
↓
[leads into Move 2]
```

---

## Move 2: Establishing a Niche

**Purpose:** Create a justification for the current research by identifying what is missing, unknown, problematic, or unresolved in the existing literature.

This is the most critical Move. Without a clear niche, the Introduction lacks a rhetorical reason for existing.

### Four niche strategies (choose one):

**Step 1A — Counter-claiming**
Challenge or qualify prior findings.
- *"However, these studies have focused exclusively on X, neglecting Y."*
- *"Despite these advances, [limitation] remains."*
- *"While [prior work] demonstrated X, subsequent studies have questioned this claim."*

**Step 1B — Indicating a gap**
Point to something that has not been done.
- *"To date, no study has examined…"*
- *"Little attention has been paid to…"*
- *"X remains poorly understood."*
- *"The relationship between X and Y has not been investigated."*

**Step 1C — Question-raising**
Pose a research question the field has not answered.
- *"It is not yet clear whether…"*
- *"How X affects Y is still unknown."*
- *"Whether X applies to Y remains an open question."*

**Step 1D — Continuing a tradition**
Position the work as extending an existing research line.
- *"Building on [prior work], this study…"*
- *"Following the approach of [X], the present study…"*

**Tense:** Present simple or present perfect ("has not been studied", "remains unclear")
**Voice:** Active or passive

---

## Move 3: Occupying the Niche

**Purpose:** Announce what the present study does to fill the niche established in Move 2.

### Step 1A — Outlining purposes (obligatory)
State what this paper investigates, examines, proposes, or does.
- Tense: **present simple** (*"This paper investigates…"*) or infinitive (*"To address this gap, we…"*)
- Active voice preferred
- Lexical signals: *this study investigates / examines / proposes / presents / aims to / seeks to…*

### Step 1B — Announcing present research
Describe the research approach or design.
- *"Using X method, this study…"*
- *"Drawing on a corpus of…"*

### Step 2 — Announcing principal findings (optional)
Preview the main results.
- Tense: **past simple** or **present simple** depending on how findings are framed
- *"Results show that…"* / *"We find that…"*

### Step 3 — Indicating RA structure (optional)
Outline the organization of the paper.
- *"The paper is organized as follows…"*
- *"Section 2 reviews the literature; Section 3 describes the method…"*

## Common Introduction Problems

| Problem | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Move 2 missing — no gap identified | Add a specific limitation of prior work before Move 3 |
| Move 2 vague ("little research exists") | Name specific studies and their specific limitations |
| Move 1 too long — literature dump | Cut to the most relevant prior work; ensure each citation serves the niche |
| Move 3 states the field's needs, not this paper's actions | Rewrite: "This paper investigates…" not "Research is needed on…" |
| No funnel — general and specific mixed | Reorganize: broad → background → literature → gap → this study |
| Wrong tense in Move 3 | Use present simple for purpose statements, not past |
