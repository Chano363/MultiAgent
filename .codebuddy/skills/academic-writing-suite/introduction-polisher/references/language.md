# Language Features — Introduction Section

---

## Tense and Voice by Move

| Move / Step | Tense | Voice | Notes |
|-------------|-------|-------|-------|
| Move 1 — Centrality claim | Present simple | Active or passive | Topic is currently important |
| Move 1 — Background facts | Present simple | Active or passive | Established knowledge |
| Move 1 — Prior studies (specific) | Past simple | Active or passive | "Smith (2020) found…" |
| Move 1 — Prior studies (trends) | Present perfect | Active or passive | "Studies have shown…" |
| Move 2 — Gap / problem | Present simple or present perfect | Active or passive | "X remains unclear" / "has not been studied" |
| Move 3 — Purpose statement | Present simple | Active preferred | "This paper investigates…" |
| Move 3 — Findings preview | Past simple or present | Active | "Results showed…" / "We find…" |
| Move 3 — Paper structure | Present simple | Active or passive | "Section 2 reviews…" |

---

## Reporting Verbs for Literature Review (Move 1)

Choose verbs that reflect the strength of the claim:

**Strong / factual:**
*show, demonstrate, prove, establish, confirm, reveal*

**Moderate / interpretive:**
*find, suggest, indicate, report, observe, note*

**Attributive (neutral):**
*argue, claim, propose, assert, maintain, contend*

**Methodological:**
*investigate, examine, analyze, study, explore, test*

**Evaluative:**
*fail to, overlook, neglect, ignore, underestimate*

## Lexical Signals by Move

### Move 1 — Importance / centrality
- *X has attracted growing attention…*
- *X is a central concern in…*
- *In recent years, X has become increasingly important…*
- *X plays a crucial role in…*
- *Understanding X is essential for…*

### Move 1 — Literature review transitions
- *A number of studies have examined…*
- *Previous research has shown that…*
- *[Author] investigated / found / reported that…*
- *Several researchers have argued that…*

### Move 2 — Gap / niche signals
- *However, / Despite this, / Nevertheless,*
- *To date, no study has…*
- *Little is known about…*
- *X remains poorly understood.*
- *The relationship between X and Y has received little attention.*
- *Previous studies have focused on X, but Y has been neglected.*
- *There is a lack of research on…*
- *It is not yet clear whether…*

### Move 3 — Purpose / announcement
- *This paper investigates / examines / explores / analyzes…*
- *The aim of this study is to…*
- *This study seeks to / aims to…*
- *To address this gap, the present study…*
- *The present research focuses on…*
- *We propose / present / develop…*

### Move 3 — Paper structure
- *The paper is organized as follows…*
- *The remainder of this paper is structured as follows…*
- *Section 2 provides… / Section 3 describes…*
- *The paper proceeds as follows…*

---

## Hedging in Move 1 (Literature Evaluation)

When evaluating prior work, use hedges to avoid overclaiming:
- *These findings suggest that…* (not "prove")
- *The results appear to indicate…*
- *This may be explained by…*
- *It seems that prior studies have overlooked…*

Do not hedge Move 3 purpose statements — state clearly what the paper does.

---

## Common Language Errors

- ❌ Using past tense for Move 3 purpose: "This paper investigated…" (should be present: "investigates")
- ❌ Using present tense for specific past studies: "Smith finds that…" (should be: "Smith found that…" or "Smith (2020) argues that…")
- ❌ Move 2 without hedging: "No research exists on X" (usually overclaiming; hedge: "little research has examined X")
- ❌ Passive in Move 3 announcement: "X is investigated in this paper" (prefer active: "This paper investigates X")
- ❌ Vague purpose: "This paper discusses X" (too vague; specify: "This paper analyzes the effect of X on Y using Z method")
