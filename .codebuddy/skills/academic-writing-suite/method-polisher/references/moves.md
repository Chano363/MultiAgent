# Method Section — Move-Step Framework

Based on Swales & Feak (1994) and the textbook's Table 9.1 Move-Step Analysis.

---

## Move 1: Describing Subjects / Data Sources

**Purpose:** Identify and characterize what or who was studied, and justify the selection.

### Steps:

**1a — Introducing the sampling of subjects / sources**
State the total sample size and selection method.
- *"The sample consisted of 20 children aged 3–6 years old."*
- *"Data were collected from 150 participants recruited via…"*
- *"A corpus of 50 research articles was compiled from…"*

**1b — Introducing background of subjects**
Provide relevant characteristics (demographics, experience, background).
- *"There were 14 male and 6 female subjects."*
- *"Participants were undergraduate students with no prior experience in…"*

**1c — Informing about the moral code of research**
State ethical procedures: consent, anonymity, risk, IRB approval.
- *"All participants provided informed written consent."*
- *"The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of…"*
- *"Participants received no compensation for their involvement."*

**1d — Introducing the setting of subjects**
Describe where subjects were located or how they were situated.
- *"The study took place in two preschool classrooms…"*
- *"Data were collected at three urban secondary schools in…"*

---

## Move 2: Describing the Experiment / Procedure

**Purpose:** Describe what was done in sufficient detail for replication.

### Steps (experimental research):

**2a — Introducing experimental techniques**
Name the overall research design and approach.
- *"A between-subjects experimental design was used."*
- *"An observational study was conducted over a 6-week period."*

**2b — Introducing experimental variables**
Define independent and dependent variables, and how they were operationalized.
- *"The independent variable was X, manipulated at three levels: …"*
- *"The dependent variable was operationalized as…"*

**2c — Introducing instruments / equipment / materials**
Describe tools, questionnaires, stimuli, software, or apparatus.
- *"A validated 20-item Likert scale was used to measure…"*
- *"Each investigator was equipped with a stopwatch to…"*
- *"The experiment was conducted using [software name], version X."*

**2d — Introducing tasks**
Describe what participants did.
- *"Participants were asked to…"*
- *"Each child was observed for 30 minutes during free play."*

**2e — Describing procedure of experiment**
Detail the step-by-step sequence of the experiment in chronological order.
- Must be specific enough to allow replication
- Include timing, sequence, randomization, counterbalancing

### Steps (empirical/observational research):

For survey, interview, corpus, or observational studies, Move 2 covers:
- Instrument description (survey items, interview protocol, observation checklist)
- Administration procedure (how and when data was collected)
- Controls for bias or confounds

## Move 3: Describing the Data

**Purpose:** Characterize the data collected and explain how it was recorded and coded.

### Steps:

**3a — Introducing criteria of data collection**
State what qualified as valid data and what was excluded.
- *"Only responses from participants who completed all items were included."*
- *"Utterances shorter than 5 words were excluded from analysis."*

**3b — Introducing instrument of data collection**
Name the recording method or coding system.
- *"Observations were recorded using a binary coding system (0 = absent, 1 = present)."*
- *"Audio recordings were transcribed verbatim."*

**3c — Describing procedure of data collection**
Explain how data was gathered in practice.
- *"Each researcher observed five children over a 30-minute period."*

**3d — Indicating type of data**
Specify quantitative, qualitative, or mixed; nominal, ordinal, interval, ratio.

**3e — Indicating size of data**
State the total amount of data collected.
- *"A total of 1,200 responses were collected."*
- *"The corpus comprised 50,000 words across 30 texts."*

---

## Move 4: Introducing Method of Analysis

**Purpose:** Explain how data was analyzed and what criteria were used.

### Steps:

**4a — Defining concept / terminology**
Define key analytical terms or constructs.
- *"Inter-rater reliability was operationalized as percent agreement above 90%."*

**4b — Introducing criteria of analysis**
State the thresholds, frameworks, or standards applied.
- *"Statistical significance was set at p < .05."*
- *"Coding followed the framework proposed by [Author]."*

**4c — Introducing procedure of analysis**
Describe the analytical steps.
- *"Data were analyzed using SPSS version 26."*
- *"Descriptive statistics were calculated for each variable, followed by…"*
- *"Thematic analysis was conducted in six phases following Braun and Clarke (2006)."*

**4d — Setting up framework of analysis**
For qualitative studies, explain the coding scheme or analytical lens.
- *"Responses were coded inductively into categories, then grouped into themes."*

## Variation by Research Type

| Research type | Move 1 emphasis | Move 2 emphasis | Move 3 emphasis | Move 4 emphasis |
|--------------|----------------|----------------|----------------|----------------|
| Experimental (lab) | Sample description | Procedure, controls, variables | Recording method | Statistical tests |
| Survey / questionnaire | Participant recruitment | Instrument description, administration | Response data, completeness | Statistical analysis |
| Observational | Setting, subjects | Observation protocol, timing | Coding system | Inter-rater reliability |
| Corpus / text | Corpus compilation criteria | Selection and annotation process | Corpus size, type | Analytical framework |
| Computational | Dataset description | Algorithm / model | Dataset size | Evaluation metrics |

---

## Common Errors

- ❌ Describing the sample but not the selection rationale
- ❌ Naming instruments without describing their properties or validation
- ❌ Describing procedure out of chronological order
- ❌ Failing to define key variables operationally
- ❌ "Data were analyzed" without specifying how
- ❌ Present tense throughout (Method uses past tense — it happened already)
- ❌ Active voice as default ("We observed children by…" vs. "Children were observed through…")
